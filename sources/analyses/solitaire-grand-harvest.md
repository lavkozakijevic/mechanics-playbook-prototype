## Core loop and automation

**O10. Solitaire level rules.**

**Observed:** Each level is a solitaire layout in which the user plays face-up cards one higher or one lower than the current base card, and draws a new base card from the deck when no card is playable ("No playable cards, tap the deck to deal a new card") (tier: directly observed). A level ends as won when the layout is cleared, and up to three stars are awarded, with unused cards from the extra stack counting toward the star bar (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The second level teaches play in descending order as well as ascending order; the narrator's guess that direction depends on card color is not stated by the app (tier: plausible, narrator's inference).
- A level win pays credits, observed as 1,000 on the first level and a small amount on later levels relative to the entry cost (tier: directly observed).
- Stars do not visibly change the credits paid for a level in the narrator's account (tier: plausible, one comparison only).

*Cross-reference: O12, O20, O38.*

**Detail:**

- A different layout, "Play the leftmost cards to scroll, reach the far right to remove all cards", is used in the gem tutorial level (tier: directly observed).
- The narrator reports levels getting harder around level 11 and easier again afterwards (tier: directly observed).

# Pass three: proposed new tags

### Interval Claim

**Draft definition.** An interval claim is a free benefit that the product makes claimable again after a fixed interval of real time has passed since the last claim, shows the time remaining until the next claim, and grants nothing until the user returns and collects it.

**Source observations:** O63, O64.

**Conditions it appears to depend on:** A stated interval shorter than a day (one hour for the harvest, three hours for cookies); a visible countdown to the next claim; a claim action in the product; a benefit paid into a balance the product maintains.

**Why it is not covered.** Passive Construction requires a persistent entity that moves from a pre-construction state to a completed state through an active construction instance that advances without repeated input. The harvest timer builds nothing: when it runs out, the existing crops can be harvested again for credits, and neither the crops nor the farm change state. The entry's exclusions name both halves of this case: an existing structure that passively generates resources with no construction under way, and a timer that only delays reuse without creating or upgrading a persistent entity. The crops themselves grow through level completion (O16), not through the timer, and the cookie bonus (O64) has no entity at all. Daily Login Rewards requires a reward created by accessing the product within a defined daily window, limited to one entitlement per window, with a new opportunity at the daily reset. Here the qualifying condition is elapsed time since the last claim rather than access within a day, which the entry excludes; the harvest pays every hour and the cookie bonus every three hours, so several claims fall in one day, which the entry's daily claim limit also excludes; and both countdowns were observed starting at the claim (the cookie countdown read two hours fifty-nine minutes directly after it), which points to a clock that restarts from the last claim rather than a daily boundary. What the two share is a claimable reward that recurs; what Daily Login Rewards defines is a calendar of daily windows, and even a wall-clock version of these timers would run on intervals shorter than a day, which that entry does not cover. Energy covers a capacity consumed by actions, and nothing is consumed here. Daily / Weekly Quests requires an objective beyond returning.

**Recurrence elsewhere:** Timed free collections of this kind are common in mobile games, such as resource collectors and free chests on timers; the pattern can be described without reference to Solitaire Grand Harvest.

**Caveat.** This proposal is held open. The split that decides its shape was not observed: whether unclaimed benefits accumulate across intervals up to a cap while the user is away, or whether the claim pays once and then waits until it is collected. A timer that pays once and waits is a different mechanic from one that banks up to a cap during absence, and the definition above cannot be settled until one of them is shown. Two kinds of evidence would settle it: a session that leaves the app for longer than several intervals and then returns to the harvest or the cookie bonus, or a second app carrying the same shape in which the accumulation rule is visible.

# Close: system view

Credits sit at the center of Solitaire Grand Harvest. Every level costs credits to enter (O12), and the multiplier raises both the cost and every reward the level pays (O13), so the stake the user chooses scales the whole economy at once: credits, gems, cookies, puzzle pieces and pack quality (O34, O35, O40, O43). Level wins pay back a small amount relative to the entry cost (O10), alongside a wide set of free faucets: level gifts (O18), Daily Goodies (O61), the hourly harvest (O63), trail and puzzle rewards (O21, O28), set rewards (O26) and the lucky wheel (O42).

The main sink beyond entry is the paid continuation at the end of the deck (O38), where prices rise within a level and across the session. This is the point at which the balance was seen to run short, and the point at which timed offers opened mid-level (O54). The timed offers (O53), the store (O52), the Grand Kit (O55), the paid wheel spin (O57) and the puzzle piece sale (O58) all sell into the same balances. Peggy (O56) is filled by every level played, won or lost, and becomes purchasable at a fixed price once full, taking over the offer slot when the first offers expire (O54).

The farm runs on gems collected inside levels (O35), so farm progress (O23), the Bloomlight Garden season (O24) and the balloon bonus (O25) all require more levels to be played, and the season text ties more gems to a higher multiplier. Farm tasks grant XP (O23), farm levels open new areas (O31), and a larger farm raises the daily delivery bonus (O65), which pays credits back into the same balance.

Around the level loop, the app layers progress tracks that each take a by-product of play: My Trail takes mission energy (O21, O22), Crop Master takes stars (O20), the pet takes cookies (O29), the puzzle takes pieces (O28), the bones meter takes consecutive wins (O19), Cheese Rally takes won levels against a race clock (O48), and Grand Emblems takes emblems from those events (O27). Most of these carry their own countdowns (O66), so a single won level advances several clocks at once. The album (O26) is filled by packs won in levels (O43) and sold in the store (O52), and its duplicates feed the Crown Center (O41).

Access is staged by player level (O30): each new system arrives at a stated level, so the balances and sinks grow as the user advances, and the notification request (O60), the rating prompt (O62) and the newsletter request (O51) are placed after offers, rewards and long play. The return side combines daily (O61, O65), hourly (O63) and three-hourly (O64) claims with multi-day event countdowns (O66), so claims and deadlines fall due at several time scales. Social reach is thin in the session: friends, teams and trading are present as surfaces or gates (O45, O46, O47), and the only outward mechanic observed is the invite link (O50).
