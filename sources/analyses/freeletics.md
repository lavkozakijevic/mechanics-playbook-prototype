# Freeletics: App Analysis

**Session date:** 25 Feb 2026

**Additional sessions:** None.

**As observed:** 25 Feb 2026

**App version:** None.

**Analysis date:** 15 Sep 2026

**Last updated:** 15 Sep 2026


**Source.** This analysis is built from one Granola walkthrough session, "Freeletics" (25 Feb 2026), retrieved as a full transcript. It is the only Granola session for this app. The session covers the launch screens, the data consent and tracking requests, the twelve-step onboarding questionnaire, account creation and email confirmation, the reminders request, the first paywall, the training journey setup, the discounted offer that follows it, the Coach tab and its workout catalogue, the profile with its level, rewards, statistics and leaderboard, the referral surface, and the Community tab with its feed, challenges and network. No workout is started and no purchase is made. The transcript is machine speech-to-text of the narrator reading the screens aloud: the app name is rendered several ways ("freely", "forletics", "frilletics"), "paywall" is rendered "payroll" and "save" is rendered "say", and some passages are garbled. Where a garbled passage bears on a claim, the claim is tiered accordingly.

# Pass three: proposed new tags

Three proposals were made on this run and all three were ruled on on 15 Sep 2026. None was approved. Plan Generation Reveal and Guided Program are held as open proposals and stay below with what would settle each. Permission Pre-Prompt was rejected on scope and is recorded under Entries considered and not applied.

### Plan Generation Reveal

**Draft definition.** After collecting the user's answers about themselves and their goals, the product shows an animated interval presented as the system building a plan for that user, then presents what follows as the personalized result of those answers, at a point where the user has not yet received anything from the product and the next step asks for an account, a commitment or a payment.

**Source observations:** O12, O17, O3, O11, O43.

**Conditions it appears to depend on:** a preceding questionnaire whose stated purpose is to build the plan, observed as the AI coach framing at O3; an interval presented as construction rather than as loading, observed twice, as the AI coach designing the plan at O12 and as building the personalized training plan at O17; and placement immediately before an ask, observed as account creation after O12 and the half-price offer after O17.

**Why it is not covered.** Future Projection requires a position the product holds and a computed value at a stated horizon; the graph at O12 carries neither, and the construction interval is a separate element from it. Personal Data Reflection returns recorded data as steps and excludes profiling questionnaires. Challenge, Milestone and Progression Gate concern objectives, recognition and access rather than the presentation of a generated result. No entry classifies an interval staged as the system working on the user's behalf.

**Recurrence elsewhere:** subscription onboarding in fitness, nutrition, language learning, meditation and personal finance products commonly stages a "building your plan" or "analysing your answers" interval between a questionnaire and a paywall. Gymverse O19 records a plan built behind a run of messages at the end of onboarding and is a candidate second instance; it has not been assessed against this definition, including whether an ask follows it. It is describable without reference to this app.

**Caveat.** Held as an open proposal on 15 Sep 2026, not rejected. The placement is established: the interval occurs twice, each time immediately before an account request or a payment (O12, O13, O17, O43) (tier: directly observed). The interval stages effort the session gives no evidence of the product performing, since nothing shows the plan or the graph varying with the answers (tier: plausible). What holds it back is scope: as observed, the interval changes how a moment in the flow is presented rather than what the user does afterwards or returns for, which places it close to presentation rather than to a mechanic. Two things would settle it. First, a second instance in another product, assessed against this definition, showing the same construction interval placed immediately before an ask. Second, evidence that anything in the user's behaviour afterwards, such as creating the account, continuing to the paywall or subscribing, can be attributed to the wait rather than to the plan it presents, for example the same flow compared with and without the interval. Without the second, the pattern stays presentation and should be recorded in plain language. The entry must not assert that the interval corresponds to any computation.

### Guided Program

**Draft definition.** The product asks the user to enrol in one of several pre-built, named programs from a catalogue, each stated as a number of sessions across a number of weeks, then asks which days of the week the user will train, with days preselected and changeable, and lays the chosen program's sessions onto a calendar the user sees on the main screen.

**Source observations:** O15, O17, O19, O6.

**Conditions it appears to depend on:** a choice among named programs with stated size and duration, observed as three journeys of 18 sessions in six weeks, 48 in twelve and 40 in twelve; a required selection before the main screen is reached; a weekly schedule set by the user with a default, observed as Wednesday, Friday and Sunday preselected; and a standing calendar of what is next, observed on the Coach tab.

**Why it is not covered.** Challenge requires a success condition and an end boundary that conclude as completed or not, and no such state or framing is observed for the journeys. Daily / Weekly Quests requires objectives issued or refreshed on a cadence with their own completion states. Milestone requires recognition of a reached point. Seasonal Progression Pass requires reward thresholds. None classifies enrolment in a fixed-length pre-built program with a user-set weekly schedule.

The Gymverse analysis proposed Prescribed Plan Schedule, which was rejected because its one session crossed no period boundary and the behavior might be Daily / Weekly Quests. That is a different pattern from this one. Gymverse generates a schedule from constraints the user states during onboarding, a training frequency and a scheduled or flexible choice (Gymverse O11, O12, O28). Freeletics offers pre-built journeys from a catalogue, each with its own stated size and duration, and the user picks one before setting training days (O15, O17). The calendar is only the surface on which both products place their output, and the calendar alone does not make them the same pattern. The Gymverse rejection therefore does not dispose of this proposal, though the Daily / Weekly Quests question it turned on is equally open here.

**Recurrence elsewhere:** structured training plans in running and strength products, couch-to-5k style programs, fixed-length meditation courses and language courses with a weekly study schedule take this shape. It is describable without reference to this app.

**Caveat.** Held as an open proposal on 15 Sep 2026, not rejected. It rests on one instance, and no journey was entered: no session is started, completed, missed or rescheduled, and whether the journey ends, extends or restarts is unknown (O22) (tier: directly observed). The distinction that separates it from Prescribed Plan Schedule, a catalogue of pre-built programs rather than a schedule generated from stated constraints, is visible in this app's setup screens but cannot be established as a recurring pattern from one app. Three things would settle it. First, a second product offering pre-built, fixed-length programs from a catalogue, which establishes the catalogue-versus-generated distinction as a pattern rather than a feature of one product. Second, a session in which a journey is entered and a scheduled day passes, showing whether sessions are issued or refreshed on a period rule, which would bring the behavior under Daily / Weekly Quests, or conclude as completed or not at the journey's end, which would bring it under Challenge. Third, whether the program is a designed layer over the product's service or the service itself, which if the latter returns it to plain language in the core loop section.

# Close: system view

Freeletics is built as a funnel that turns a self-description into a plan, and the plan into a price. The questionnaire at O3 to O11 is framed from its first screen as input to an "AI coach", and every screen in it asks for something the product can call personal: body and identity at O4, goals over twelve weeks at O6, fitness level at O8, modality at O9, motivation at O10.
