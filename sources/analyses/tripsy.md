## Onboarding and first run

**O1. Value proposition carousel.**

**Observed:** On first launch Tripsy opens with "Welcome to Tripsy, the ultimate travel companion" and a Get started button (tier: directly observed). The welcome is the first of seven swipeable screens, each presenting one capability (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Screen one, Welcome: stay organized on your trips without missing a thing (tier: directly observed).
- Screen two, Itinerary planner: all travel information in one place, as a detailed itinerary by day and time, a list of activities, or both (tier: directly observed).
- Screen three, Flight details: flight updates via push notifications so a gate or time change is never missed (tier: directly observed).
- Screen four, Collaboration: share a trip with guests, who can collaborate for free (tier: directly observed).
- Screen five, Automation: forward flight tickets, hotel reservations and car rentals to save them to the itinerary (tier: directly observed).
- Screen six, Expenses: manage expenses from flights and accommodation to meals and activities, framed as staying on budget (tier: directly observed).
- Screen seven, Documents: store travel documents from images and notes to videos, all available offline (tier: directly observed).

## Applied tags

**Tag:** Set Collection

**Observations:** O29

**Supporting observations:** O27, O28, O30

**Confidence:** strongly supported

**Role:** retention

The set fills across separate trips over time rather than within one session, which the book's cumulative statistics in O28 and the year-by-year trip view in O27 place on the scale of repeated use.

**Rationale:** Exclusions were checked first. Tripsy tracks which distinct members of a defined set have been visited, not a total of interchangeable items, so the cumulative-count exclusion does not apply (tier: strongly supported). The set is defined as 250 countries and territories, Show visited lists every country and marks which ones the user has visited, and the book reports the visited count against the world total inside a progress ring (O29) (tier: directly observed). The product frames the count as completeness of a world total, which meets the presence standard's product-framing test and the engagement-design test as a record of progressing (tier: strongly supported). The count stood at zero and no member was seen changing state, so the registration rule is known only from the settings copy, which counts a country reached by flight or train when Count transit countries is on (O30) (tier: directly observed).

**Variants present:** Single-set collection: one defined set of countries and territories (O29) (tier: directly observed).

**Variants not established:** Completion-tier collection; hidden-member collection; shared collection, including whether trips shared with the user count toward the user's set (O39); renewable collection; time-limited collection; whether the city display in O30 forms a second tracked set.

**Alternative considered:** A travel statistics display that reports places visited without the product treating completeness as something to advance, which would carry no label. The progress ring against a world total and the per-country visited list favor Set Collection. Seeing the count and the list change when a trip to a new country is recorded would separate the two.

**Tag:** Group Membership

**Observations:** O38, O39, O40

**Supporting observations:** O1, O41, O42, O61

**Confidence:** strongly supported

**Role:** social

O38 and O40 place other identified people in named roles on the user's trip, and O42 and O61 concern what those people add to it.

**Rationale:** Tripsy treats a shared trip as a group context with a maintained roster: Manage guests lists the people traveling, starting with the owner (O40), and membership is created by invitation (O38) (tier: directly observed). Product copy states that membership changes what each person can do and see: view-only guests can see but not add, edit or remove content, collaborators can edit and may be given the right to invite and remove guests, and Traveling together decides whether the trip appears in a guest's My Trips and travel log (O38, O39) (tier: directly observed). No invited guest joined during the session, so these effects are stated by the product rather than seen operating, which holds confidence below directly observed (tier: directly observed). The sibling decision ladder was applied. No collective group-owned state was observed: the trip is presented as the owner's, with the owner as admin, and items are attributed to the guests who added them (O8, O40, O42), so Clan / Guild does not apply (tier: strongly supported). Maintained membership and governance are present, which selects this label over Community Space (tier: directly observed).

**Variants present:** Invitation-only group (O38) (tier: directly observed). Role-based group, with View only and Collaborator roles (O38) (tier: directly observed). Delegated-membership group, where a collaborator can be given Manage guests (O38) (tier: directly observed). Shared-resource group, where members reach the trip's itinerary, documents and expenses under per-role permissions (O38) (tier: strongly supported).

**Variants not established:** Activity-bounded or time-bounded group, including whether membership ends with the trip; public or hidden group; communication group; whether a guest's contributions stay on the trip after the guest leaves; what the friends list in O39 does for guests not traveling together.

**Alternative considered:** Community Space, which would apply to an open shared context without maintained roles; the roster, invitation rule and permission roles rule it out under the decision ladder. Clan / Guild, which would apply if the trip held a state belonging to its members as a collective; evidence that the trip's records or totals are shown as the group's and persist after the owner leaves would be needed to select it.

# Close: system view

**Observed dependencies.**

Tripsy is organized around one object, the trip (tier: directly observed). First run offers three ways into it (O3): creating a trip (O6), forwarding a reservation (O46), or opening a populated sample (O4) (tier: directly observed). The Pro offer precedes all three (O2, O52), and a Pro pop-up follows the creation of the first own trip (O56) (tier: directly observed).

Forwarding feeds the automation that turns emails into itinerary entries, documents and expense amounts (O21), with unparsed emails held for attaching (O22) (tier: directly observed). Those entries then feed the conflict warning (O23), the day-by-day itinerary with its weather and leave-time rows (O16), and the type-specific lists and maps (O13) (tier: directly observed). Activities added by hand through search (O9) feed the same lists, routes (O12) and expense totals (O18) (tier: directly observed). Saved addresses (O24) feed the leave-time row (O16) (tier: directly observed).

The social layer depends on a trip to share. Group Membership (O38, O39, O40) rests on the trip, guest attribution (O42) and collaboration alerts (O61) depend on guests joining, and incoming invitations surface in Pending invites (O41) (tier: directly observed). Web links (O44) depend on an account (O36), and account creation is where the notification request appears (O60), which the alerts in O61 and O62 depend on (tier: directly observed).

My Tripsy Book (O28) and the Set Collection over countries (O29) depend on trips accumulating over time, under the counting rules in O30 (tier: strongly supported). The book is the source of the Shareable Win images (O28), and its recap (O31) and advanced stats (O34) remain unreached (tier: directly observed).

Pro runs across the whole structure. Locks touch the itinerary (O16, O57), documents (O17), expenses (O18), flight alerts (O61), advanced stats (O34) and app icons (O37) on the user's own free trip (O32), while the sample trip displays the same sections open (O33) (tier: directly observed). Pro cards sit on My Trips, in trips, in activity details and in Settings (O55), and the release notes lead to the Pro page (O58) (tier: directly observed). Reservation forwarding, listed under Pro, worked in the session without the trial being started (O35) (tier: strongly supported).

**Interpretation.**

The sample trip and reservation forwarding both reduce how much a user must enter before a trip looks complete, which is consistent with the foundation's Low-Friction Entry pattern and with Starting Cost Is Too High as the obstacle it would address (tier: plausible). An alternative reading is that both mainly display paid capability at work, since the sample shows Pro sections open (O33) and forwarding sits under Pro in the comparison (O54) (tier: plausible). Evidence about what users do after opening the sample, and whether forwarding remains available to accounts that never start the trial, would separate the two. Nothing in the sessions supports a claim about effectiveness.
