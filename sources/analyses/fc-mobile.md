## Onboarding and first run

**O1. Age declaration precedes everything else.**

**Observed:** After an update download, the first screen asks for the user's birth month and year before play, and ties that entry to acceptance of the user agreement (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Copy: "please enter your birth month and year to play to accept the user agreement and everything that goes with it" (tier: directly observed).
- The control is a scroller for year and month (tier: directly observed).
- No alternative route past this screen was shown (tier: directly observed).

**O2. The tracking prompt is rendered with its accept control off screen.**

**Observed:** The system tracking prompt appears with its text and its two buttons in separately scrolling areas, so that only "Ask App not to track" is visible without deliberate swiping (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Prompt text: "allow FC Mobile to track your activity across other companies, apps and websites" (tier: directly observed).
- Swiping the text moves the text only; the buttons remain fixed (tier: directly observed).
- The user had to tap on "Ask app not to track" and swipe up to bring "Allow" into view (tier: directly observed).
- The default visible option is the declining one (tier: directly observed).

**O3. A long load precedes the first interactive screen.**

**Observed:** The loading screen persists well beyond a minute before the sign-in screen appears (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The user estimates "definitely more than 60 seconds or perhaps nearing 100 plus seconds" (tier: strongly supported, timed by the speaker rather than by the app).
- UEFA branding and the line "kicking off 97, getting warmed up" occupy the wait (tier: directly observed).
- Gameplay resources are downloaded again at the start of the tutorial and again before the first match (tier: directly observed).

**O4. Sign-in is required before play, with an unsaved guest route.**

**Observed:** A "Sign in to save your progress" screen offers five routes and cannot be dismissed; guest is one of the five (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Copy: "Login with a service of your choice to save your progress across multiple devices. If you do not want to save your progress, you may choose to log in as a guest" (tier: directly observed).
- Routes: Sign in with Apple, EA account, Guest, Game Center, Facebook (tier: directly observed).
- Closing the EA account sheet returns the user to a "tap to play" screen and then to the same sign-in screen (tier: directly observed).
- The user chose Guest; the app then displayed "welcome back", recognising prior play (tier: directly observed).

## Core loop and automation

**O25. Matches in several modes are the repeating unit.**

**Observed:** The repeating activity is playing football matches, offered across a large number of named modes reached from one screen (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Modes named: Kickoff, Head to Head, VS Attack, Division Rivals, Manager Mode, Chapters, Live Events, Skill Games, Icon Chronicles, Club Challenge, Football Center, friendly matches, league tournaments, and an Arcade named in the May session (tier: directly observed).
- Controls: a virtual stick plus shoot, sprint, tackle, pass and through-pass buttons (tier: directly observed).
- Five camera options; the user notes tapping accuracy depends on screen size (tier: plausible, the cameras are observed, the size relationship is the user's inference).
- Match sound is lost whenever the app is minimised and returns only after a full restart (tier: directly observed).
- Substitutions and in-match tactical changes are available during play (tier: directly observed).

**O26. Control passes to the system inside a match whenever the user lets go.**

**Observed:** When the user stops holding the stick, movement passes to automatic control while the action buttons remain live (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- "When the game is being played, it switches to auto very quickly. If the player isn't controlling the joystick and isn't holding joystick, then quickly goes to auto" (tier: directly observed).
- Under automatic movement the user can still trigger slide, pass, through ball, clear ball and shoot (tier: directly observed).
- Matches cannot be sped up or skipped (tier: directly observed).
- The user played one full match on automatic while away from the device (tier: directly observed).

**O27. Manager mode plays the match without the user and can be told when to stop.**

**Observed:** Manager mode substitutes tactical configuration for play, runs matches on its own, and accepts stop conditions for a run of matches (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Guide copy: "you unlock manager mode. In this mode, you act as the manager of your ultimate team, making key decisions and setting custom tactics" (tier: directly observed).
- Initial rank Amateur 3 (tier: directly observed).
- Tactics cover build-up speed, passing distance, mentality, positioning, passing tendency, cross tendency, shooting tendency, pressure, width, aggression and back line, with separate sets for control and counter-attack (tier: directly observed).
- "You can use one of the preset tactics or create your own... When you modify one of the predefined tactics, it will be saved as your custom tactics" (tier: directly observed).
- An "auto" control opens "automate settings" with "select stop conditions if the match rewards are reached 0 to 5" and a division-reached condition, then save and start (tier: directly observed).
- The mode then searches for a match and plays it out while the user watches; tactics can be edited mid-match; the match cannot be sped up or skipped (tier: directly observed).
- A manager mode match could not be shared to the league, unlike a head-to-head match (tier: directly observed).

**O28. Head to head matches are played against another identified player in real time.**

**Observed:** Head to head pairs the user with another player for a live match (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Guide copy: "in Head to Head you will be able to play 90 minute matches against opponents in real time" (tier: directly observed).
- Before kickoff the opponent's attack, midfield and defense are shown relative to the user's, but not their overall (tier: directly observed).
- The user cannot decline a drawn opponent (tier: directly observed).
- Emotes can be sent to the opponent, or the opponent muted (tier: directly observed).
- Ping is displayed top right, moving between 30 and 40 milliseconds (tier: directly observed).
- After the match: add friend, share with league, report, and goal replays (tier: directly observed).

**O29. Skill games are short drills scored against three star thresholds.**

**Observed:** Skill games set a point target with three thresholds determining a one-, two- or three-star result (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- "We have three targets. 3,000, 4,000 or 5,000 points. That depends on which star we're gonna get" (tier: directly observed).

- Each carries a timer and a stated number of attempts (tier: directly observed).
- Results paid a pack token, XP, two new formations and a level up (tier: directly observed).
- The user replayed a two-star result twice to reach three stars (tier: directly observed).
- A replay is not offered immediately at the result screen: "we cannot replace straight away" (tier: directly observed).

**O30. Drills carry an attempt counter whose rules were not shown.**

**Observed:** Drills display a limited number of attempts alongside the timer (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**

# Pass three: proposed new tags

Six proposals were made on this run and all six were ruled on on 14 Sep 2026. Purchase Ladder was approved, is now an approved library entry, and is applied as a tag in Pass two against the observations that produced it, O99, O100 and O101. Delegated Auto-Play, Unlock-Triggered Guided Tour and Reward Reveal Ceremony were rejected on scope and are recorded above under entries considered and not applied, each with the reasoning that rules it out in future analyses. Real-Fixture Settlement and Paid Clue Reveal were held rather than rejected: each named something real but defined it around its commercial surface rather than the behavior underneath, and each is restated below under a new name, rewritten around the behavior, and left open pending a second observed instance in another app.

### Outside Event Tie-In

**Draft definition.** The product attaches itself to an event it does not run and cannot control, invites the user to commit to a position on that event before it happens, and resolves the commitment when the world resolves the event. What the product supplies is the stake and the wait; the reward attached to the outcome is incidental to the pattern and may be small or absent.

**Source observations:** O36, O49, O98

**Conditions it appears to depend on:** an external event with a known date the product tracks; a commitment the user records before that date, such as a prediction, a selection, a side taken, or an entry; a deadline set by the external event rather than by the product's own clock; and a resolution the product reports once the event has happened. In the observed implementation the product listed real fixtures, gave "13 hours left to choose who's the winner", let the user play the fixture in-product in the interval, and stated that rewards follow "based on real life match results" (O36). A purchased thirty-day reward calendar was separately pinned to the date of a named final (O98).

**Why it is not covered.** Challenge covers a bounded objective whose success condition the product itself evaluates; here the condition is settled outside the product by something the user cannot influence, and the boundary belongs to a third party's calendar. Variable Reward Outcome covers a resolution the system performs. Daily Claim Pack and Daily Login Rewards cover intervals the product defines. No entry covers the product borrowing an external event's schedule and suspense.

**Recurrence elsewhere:** the pattern does not need a game or a sport. A bank running a promotion against a tournament, a fitness product built around a race date, a news product on election night, a retail product on a results announcement, and a trading product on a scheduled release would all show it. It is describable without reference to this app.

**Caveat.** Held pending a second instance. Only one product has been observed running it, and within that product no commitment was seen resolving: the duel's settlement rule was read from copy rather than watched operating (O36). Before this can be written as an entry it needs a second observed instance in another app, and at least one commitment seen through to its resolution, so that the boundary against Challenge can be drawn on behavior rather than on framing. If settlement turns out to depend only on the user's own in-product play, the pattern collapses into a bounded objective with a themed deadline.

### Progressive Reveal

**Draft definition.** The product withholds something it already holds and discloses it in pieces rather than at once, so that the user returns or acts again to see more of it. The pattern is the drip: what the product charges for a piece, if anything, is incidental, and a reveal released on a schedule is the clearer case because nothing but the wait moves it.

**Source observations:** O39, O49, O63

**Conditions it appears to depend on:** a withheld item, result, or content set the product has already determined; a stated or implied scope for what is being withheld, such as a pool size or a number of pieces; a route by which one piece is released, whether a timer, an action, or a payment; and a state that persists between releases so the user can see how much remains. In the observed implementations a mystery player item was narrowed clue by clue from a stated pool of 154 (O39), event theme challenges stated "reveals in five days" (O49), and chapter entries stated "unlocks at week three" and "unlocks at week four" (O63).

**Why it is not covered.** Loot Box and Variable Reward Outcome classify a resolution that happens at once when a commitment is made; this pattern is the opposite shape, a resolution spread deliberately across time or across actions. Progression Gate classifies a part withheld until a measure the user advances reaches a value; here nothing the user does needs to advance, and in the scheduled form the user cannot advance it at all. Season Content Pass classifies an entitlement over grouped content rather than the pacing of its disclosure.

**Recurrence elsewhere:** it recurs wherever a product holds more than it shows, including staged content calendars, teased features, phased result announcements, and serialized releases. It is describable without reference to this app.

**Caveat.** Held pending a second instance. The paid form and the scheduled form were both seen inside this one product, which is not enough to establish that they are one pattern rather than two, and neither was seen operating: no clue was bought and no scheduled reveal was reached (O39, O63). Before this can be written as an entry it needs a second observed instance in another app, and preferably a scheduled reveal watched through at least two of its releases, so the entry can be built on the drip rather than on the price.

# Close: system view

FC Mobile runs one account-level progression and then hangs almost everything else off it. XP from matches, missions, drafts and skill games moves an account level (O44), and that level is what releases the product in pieces: leagues and football center at 4, challenge mode at 5, scouting camp at 6, a lineup slot at 8, the market at 9, club challenge at 10 (O45, O61). Because the level is fed by every activity at once, the sequence in which the product opens is not something the user chooses; it follows from playing at all. Each release then fires a scripted tour that leaves one control live until it is followed (O24), so the first use of every feature is performed under direction. The user's own summary of the result is that the product's surface is enormous and their understanding of it is shallow, which is the predictable consequence of an unlock schedule that outruns explanation.

The squad is the object all of that progression acts on. Player items are acquired through six distinct routes, held between matches, assigned to a limited number of positions, and developed through training that consumes other players and rank ups that consume a purchased material (O31, O32, O33, O56). This is why the monetization surfaces converge where they do: rank up points appear in six store forms (O108), packs and drafts sell the players themselves (O57, O102), and the transfer market prices them in the billions (O74). Every currency display is a route into the store section that sells it (O110), so the shortest path from any point in the product to a purchase is one tap.

Three cadences carry the return pressure, and they are deliberately not aligned. A daily layer pays for opening the app at all (O112, O113) and for four refreshable tasks (O41). A weekly layer runs the head-to-head leaderboard countdown (O82). A seasonal layer runs the Star Pass, the division ladder, the league season and the badge collections, each on its own timer of 15 to 78 days (O47, O46, O54, O50). Because the timers do not share boundaries, there is always one closing, and the product tells the user so on nearly every screen (O114) without explaining what closing means (O121). The one thing missing from this layer is continuity: no surface anywhere was observed treating a missed day as different from a slow one, and the single feature carrying the word "streak" turned out to be a purchase ladder with no continuity condition at all (O99).

The social layer is where the product's collective state lives and where it is most exposed. A league holds its own level, points, hundred-position reward track, token currency, store, tournament and two leaderboard positions (O76, O78, O70, O79, O80), and every one of those advances only through members' combined activity (O77). Several league quests name actions that individuals would otherwise have no reason to take, including watching advertisements and placing market orders, which routes the product's advertising and market surfaces through peer expectation rather than through the individual's own incentives. The design assumes an active roster; the sessions show what happens without one, with a 42-member league whose quests were stalled and a self-created league whose tournament had one signup (O77, O79). The sharing routes sit on top of this: a finished match can be posted into the league for members to watch (O40, O85), while the league's own record is the only thing the product composes for an audience outside it (O85).

Two structures depend on things the product does not control. The duels settle against real fixtures on a real calendar (O36), and the UCL final countdown pack ties a thirty-day purchased reward calendar to a named match date (O98). These import a deadline no in-product timer could manufacture, and they are the clearest case in the record of the product borrowing pressure from the world rather than generating it.

The layers feed each other in one direction more than the other. Play produces XP, which produces levels, which release features, which produce more places to play; play also produces Star Pass credits, league points, event tokens and six other balances, each with its own store (O65). What the record does not show is the reverse: nothing observed makes the paid surfaces produce play. The monthly cards pay their daily amounts whether or not the user does anything (O97), the UCL calendar is stated to unlock regardless of login (O98), and the purchase ladders advance only by paying (O99, O100, O101). That asymmetry is the structural fact this analysis can support, and it is a description of how the pieces connect rather than a claim about what any of it achieves.
