# Pass two: tagging

## Applied tags

**Tag:** Soft Currency
**Observations:** O34
**Supporting observations:** O10, O11, O14, O16, O17
**Confidence:** directly observed, the wrench balance is seen rising from match-3 wins and falling by each task's stated cost
**Role:** engagement
O16 and O17 show wrenches linking the two in-session activities, sending the user from the car to the board and back within one session.
**Rationale:** Wrenches meet every required condition: a quantified balance (O9, O34), a repeatable non-paid acquisition path through winning levels (O14, O16), stored units that carry between activities, deliberately selected exchanges for car tasks at stated prices (O11, O17), and a balance reduction on each exchange (O10). The app's own explainer ties the units to winning levels (O34). No paid path for wrenches is observed, so Hard Currency does not apply to this balance.
**Variants present:** Activity-earned soft currency; Single-sink soft currency, as the only observed use is car tasks; Multi-currency soft-currency system, as gold coins are also earned through play (O35).
**Variants not established:** Fixed-grant or variable-grant, since per-level amounts are not read out; Expiring, Capped-earning, Resetting, Transferable, Shared and Convertible soft currency; Dual-path soft currency, since no paid route for wrenches is seen.
**Alternative considered:** Experience Points, rejected because wrenches are spent and reduced rather than accumulated toward a threshold. Energy, rejected because the user chooses which task to buy rather than having units consumed by participating.

**Tag:** Hard Currency

# Pass three: proposed new tags

### Serialized Story Reveal

**Draft definition.** A serialized story reveal is a narrative scene that the product releases when the user completes a defined unit of progression, which advances a continuing story and ends on at least one question the product states as unresolved and carries forward to later units.
**Source observations:** O5, O7, O27, O28
**Conditions it appears to depend on:** A progression built from discrete units, such as projects, chapters or episodes; a persistent cast or narrative thread spanning those units; release of the scene tied to completing a unit rather than to time; an explicit statement in the scene that a question remains open ("that's a mystery for another time").
**Why it is not covered.** Milestone covers the recognition of reaching a point on a progression and its rewarded and unlocking variants, but not the release of narrative content that advances a continuing story and carries an open question across units. Companion does not apply because the characters are not acquired, assigned or developed. No approved entry covers story content as the payoff of completing progression.
**Recurrence elsewhere:** The Chrome Valley Customs analysis (session 03 Mar 2026) records a partial second instance. Its O29 describes a continuing story across episodes: the garage was robbed by an antagonist, Greasy Joe, the first build is framed as the chance to make things right, and the second episode hints that someone is ripping off car businesses across town and that more about Greasy Joe may be found. The second episode opens only after the first car is completed and revealed (its O24), and no time-based release of story is recorded, so the story there advances on completion rather than on time. The Greasy Joe thread is carried across the episode boundary as an open question. Two differences limit the match: story beats in Chrome Valley Customs arrive with each batch of tasks (its O17) rather than in one closing scene, and the hint about Greasy Joe is recorded at the opening of the second episode rather than stated as unresolved at the close of the first. That record also covers only one completed episode, so it does not show whether the thread is resolved, extended or dropped later. The Royal Match analysis describes its king as a narrative host; it has not been checked here for this shape.
**Caveat.** Only one project and its closing scene are observed. Whether the next project resolves or extends the open question about Mitch, whether scenes can be replayed, and whether story release ever depends on anything other than project completion are not observed. The introduction (O7) precedes any progression and is not itself a reveal under this definition. Two pieces of evidence would settle the proposal. First, a Match Creek Motors session that completes a second project and records whether the question about Mitch is carried into that project's scenes, extended, resolved or dropped, and whether the scene is released on completion alone. Second, a second app showing the same shape in full: a scene released on completing a unit that states a question as open and carries it into a later unit. Chrome Valley Customs comes closest, and a session there that completes a second episode and records whether the Greasy Joe thread is picked up at its close would settle whether it qualifies.

# Close: system view

Observation and interpretation are kept separate below: the first paragraph describes structure seen in the session, and the rest is labelled interpretation.

**Structure.** The app runs two activities joined by one currency. Match-3 levels (O22, Challenge) pay wrenches (O34, Soft Currency), and wrenches buy car tasks (O17) whose part choices are cosmetic (O18, Cosmetic Customization) (tier: directly observed). Tasks unlock further tasks, the project track pays gold coins at its boxes and at completion (O26, O27, Milestone), and completion releases a story scene and the choice of the next car (O28) (tier: directly observed). Because task costs repeatedly exceed the wrench balance, every step of the car depends on another match-3 win (O16) (tier: directly observed). The numbered levels (O30) carry the requirement for the player profile (O32, Progression Gate) and, by level 9, open the breaker selection (O23, O33, Boosters) (tier: strongly supported, the breaker unlock coincides with level 9 but no stated requirement is seen). Gold coins accumulate from every part of the loop and are sold in the shop (O35, O40, Hard Currency), while no part of the loop spends them (tier: directly observed). Hearts (O24, Lives) stand beside the board without being drawn down in this session (tier: directly observed). Achievements and leaderboards (O31, O37) sit outside the game in Game Center and connect to nothing observed inside it (tier: directly observed).

**Dependencies.** O17 depends on O34, which depends on O22. O26 and O27 depend on O17. O28 depends on O27. O25 is the final task of O17 and feeds O35 and O36. O23 depends on O30 and O33. O32 depends on O30. O40 depends on O35 for its meaning, and O35 has no observed downstream use.

**Interpretation.** The pattern the loop instantiates is Earned Access: play on the board creates the resource that opens the next car task (tier: strongly supported, O16 and O34). The project track instantiates Goal Decomposition and Visible Progress, dividing one car into priced components along a drawn track from broken to finished (tier: strongly supported, O10, O17, O26). The locked breakers and the level 14 profile instantiate Staged Disclosure and Unlocking (tier: plausible, the breaker requirement is unstated). The story scene at completion creates conditions consistent with Open Loops and Incomplete-Task Attention by stating an unresolved question (tier: plausible, O28); whether it draws the user on is not evidenced, and simple interest in the next car is an equally available reading. For gold coins, the analyst's own statements that their purpose is never explained (O35) are the only user evidence in the session, and they fit the obstacle Value Is Not Understood at the shop (tier: plausible, one analyst's statements; Spend Reluctance is the alternative, and a session showing the first gold coin price would separate them). The reviews on the listing (O2) describe the balance between match-3 and car work as a complaint for some users; these are store statements and are not evidence of behavior in this session.
