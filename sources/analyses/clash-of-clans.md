## Onboarding and first run

**O1. Store listing before install.**

**Observed:** The store page presents the app with an aggregate rating, an editorial award, a video preview, screenshots, a long description, and a banner for an event described as a new season (tier: directly observed). The page frames the product around building a village, raising a clan, and competing in clan wars (tier: directly observed, from the description text read aloud).

**Detail:**
- 4.8 stars from 2.7 million ratings (tier: directly observed).
- Editor's Choice award, age rating 13+, developer Supercell, English and 20 more languages, 634 MB install (tier: directly observed).
- The featured event on the page is named Sound of Clash, described as a new season (tier: directly observed).
- The narrator states that updates arrive a couple of times per month, sometimes three or four (tier: plausible, the narrator's characterisation of the update history shown on the page rather than a figure the page states).
- The review summary shown on the page reports that users find the game fun and engaging and appreciate its strategic gameplay, graphics, and progression (tier: directly observed).

**O2. Launch disclosure and consent sequence.**

## Core loop and automation

**O15. The repeating loop.**

**Observed:** Play settles into a repeating sequence of collecting resources, starting upgrades, training troops, attacking another village for loot, and returning to start further upgrades (tier: directly observed, recorded across all four sessions). The narrator states in session 3 that the core loop is again the battle and that it is repeated constantly (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Loot taken from an attack is the input to the next upgrade; upgrades raise storage and production, which raise what can be afforded (tier: strongly supported, repeatedly recorded as the reason for each attack).
- The loop is interrupted by waits rather than by any per-attempt limit on attacking (tier: strongly supported, the narrator repeatedly attacks in sequence and is blocked only by resources and builder availability).

*Cross-reference: O16, O23, O54.*

**O16. Builder slots limit parallel construction.**

**Observed:** The number of builder huts owned sets how many constructions can run at once, and with two builders a third upgrade cannot be started (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The message given is that all builders are busy (tier: directly observed).
- When a third upgrade is attempted the app offers to complete a running build and free a builder for one gem (tier: directly observed).
- Up to five builder huts are shown as buildable, at 250 gems each (tier: directly observed).
- In session 3 the narrator records builders are sleeping as the reason play cannot continue in the direction intended (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O6, O19, O95.*

**O17. Construction advances without further input.**

**Observed:** Once a build or upgrade is started it advances on a timer and completes without the user performing further construction actions, including while the app is closed (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- A builder figure is shown working on the structure during the process (tier: directly observed).
- On return after an absence the app lists upgrades that completed while the user was away (tier: directly observed).
- Completion changes the structure to a new persistent level with higher stated capacity or hit points (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O88.*

**O18. Timer durations lengthen with progression.**

**Observed:** Build times grow from seconds at the first levels to hours at later ones (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Town hall 2 takes 10 seconds; barracks upgrade 15 seconds (tier: directly observed).
- Town hall 3 takes 30 minutes, which the narrator notes as the longest upgrade seen so far and as one that would allow leaving the game and coming back (tier: directly observed).
- Town hall 6 takes 12 hours, described as super long (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O95.*

**O19. Speed-up offered at every wait.**

**Observed:** Every observed waiting state carries an offer to end it immediately for gems (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- One gem to finish an elixir collector, 10 gems to finish a 30-minute town hall upgrade, one gem to free a busy builder (tier: directly observed).
- In sessions 3 and 4 the narrator uses gems to complete the town hall upgrades rather than wait (tier: directly observed).
- The narrator records speeding up builds as an extremely important mechanic in this game (tier: plausible, an assessment by the narrator rather than an observed system behavior).

## Goals and progression

**O28. Town hall level as the master progression state.**

**Observed:** The town hall holds a numbered level that determines what else in the product is available, and raising it is the stated purpose of most activity (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Levels 1 to 18 are selectable as a minimum requirement when creating a clan, establishing the range of the track (tier: directly observed).
- Each upgrade screen lists the buildings it will unlock and the storage and hit point increases it will bring (tier: directly observed).
- The cost escalates sharply: 4,000 gold for level 2, 25,000 for level 5, 150,000 for level 6, and a stated one million coins for the level after 6 (tier: directly observed).
- The character's standing instruction is upgrade the town hall to unlock new things, if you're short on gold, battle for more (tier: directly observed).
- The narrator reaches town hall 4 in session 1, 5 in session 2, 6 in session 3, and 7 in session 4 (tier: directly observed).

## Access and eligibility

**O39. Town hall level gates content across the product.**

**Observed:** The town hall level is the condition on buildings, troops, spells, heroes, modes, cosmetics, and decorations alike (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Barracks upgrades require town hall 2; practice mode unlocks at town hall 4; spells at town hall 5; ranked battle at town hall 7; a hero at town hall 7; siege machines at town hall 12 (tier: directly observed).
- Barracks levels 17, 18, and 19 and dark barracks level 12 are listed as requirements for units in the army board (tier: directly observed).
- A free eagle monument decoration requires town hall 17 (tier: directly observed).
- Additional sceneries require town hall levels not yet reached (tier: directly observed).
- The narrator records being prompted and shown all the time that the town hall must keep being upgraded to unlock things (tier: strongly supported, the same gate message recurs across sessions 1 to 4).

*Cross-reference: O28, O47.*

**O40. Experience level gates a purchasable decoration.**

**Observed:** One decoration cannot be bought even when its price is held, because it carries a separate experience level requirement (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**

- The narrator notes that even holding the gold would not allow the purchase (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O32, O82.*

**O41. Clan access gated behind a repaired building and a storage upgrade.**

**Observed:** Clan features are unavailable until a ruined clan castle is rebuilt for 10,000 elixir, which itself requires raising elixir storage capacity to hold that amount (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**

# Pass three: proposed new tags

### Contestable Holdings

**Draft definition.** The product holds a user's accumulated resources in a state that other users can take a portion of through a product-sanctioned action, and gives the holder means to reduce how much is taken. Holding value is therefore a position that must be defended rather than a balance that only grows.

**Source observations:** O23, O24, O54, O55, O70.
**Conditions it appears to depend on:** a persistent accumulated balance visible to other users before they act; a product-sanctioned action by which another user removes part of it; disclosure to the attacker of how much is available; a record shown to the holder of what was taken and by whom; and means under the holder's control that change the amount taken, here defenses, walls, and layout.
**Why it is not covered.** No approved entry describes value the user holds being removed by another user. Soft Currency and Spendable Credits and Tokens cover acquisition and deliberate exchange, not involuntary transfer. Gifting covers a directed transfer initiated by the giver, the opposite direction. Clan / Guild covers collective state, not the taking of individual holdings. Leaderboard and Comparative Rank cover standing, not holdings.
**Recurrence elsewhere:** plausible. The structure is stated without reference to this product, and nothing in it depends on villages, troops, or a war theme; it requires only persistent balances and a sanctioned route for one user to take from another. Whether it recurs in practice was not tested here and cannot be settled from these transcripts.
**Caveat.** The user's own holdings were taken at least once during the sessions (O70), but the rules governing how much can be taken, what proportion is protected, and what the attacker gains relative to what the holder loses were never displayed. The proposal rests on the structure being observable, not on its parameters being known.

### Protection Window

**Draft definition.** The product sells or grants a time-bounded state during which a defined class of loss cannot occur to the user, with the remaining duration displayed continuously and stated exceptions under which the protection does not hold.

**Source observations:** O83, O89, O90.
**Conditions it appears to depend on:** a loss the user can suffer while away; a purchasable or granted state that suspends it; a visible countdown to the state's expiry; a price structure that makes longer durations cheaper per unit of time; a stacking rule; and stated exceptions that keep some loss possible.
**Why it is not covered.** No approved entry describes a purchased suspension of a loss condition. Hard Currency covers the balance the protection is bought with, not the protection. Energy and Lives cover capacities and continuation units consumed by the user's own actions, not a shield against another party. Daily Claim Pack covers entitlements that release rewards over intervals, not a state that withholds a loss.
**Recurrence elsewhere:** plausible. The pattern can be stated generically as protecting any user-held state from a defined loss for a bought period, and does not depend on this product's subject matter. It presupposes Contestable Holdings or another loss condition, so its recurrence is tied to that pattern's recurrence.
**Caveat.** The purchased form was priced and read but never bought, and the granted eight hour form was described in text rather than observed being applied (O90). What happens at expiry beyond the loss becoming possible again was not observed.

### Concurrent Work Slots

**Draft definition.** The product limits how many of its background processes can run at the same time to a number the user holds, sells additional units of that number, and offers to free an occupied slot immediately for payment.

**Source observations:** O6, O16, O19.
**Conditions it appears to depend on:** processes that advance on their own once started; a held count that caps how many may run at once; a message when the cap is reached; a purchasable route to raise the count permanently; and a separate purchasable route to end one running process and release its slot now.
**Why it is not covered.** Passive Construction covers the process, including that it may be slot-limited and accelerable, but classifies the construction instance rather than the held capacity that gates how many may exist. No approved entry classifies that capacity, which behaves differently from the process: it is owned, permanent, expandable by purchase, and applies across all processes rather than to one.
**Recurrence elsewhere:** plausible. Stated generically the pattern needs only self-advancing processes and a permanent owned limit on their concurrency, neither of which is specific to construction or to this product.
**Caveat.** The overlap with Passive Construction is real and an alternative reading is that the slot limit is simply one variant of that entry, which already lists slot-limited construction as a variant. The case for a separate entry is that the slot count is a distinct owned and purchasable object, not a property of any one construction. This proposal should be weighed against amending Passive Construction instead.

### Holding Ceiling

**Draft definition.** The product caps how much of a resource the user may hold in an upgradeable container, so that acquiring the next advance requires first raising the cap, and value arriving above the cap is converted into another resource rather than kept or lost.

**Source observations:** O54, O55, O59.
**Conditions it appears to depend on:** a cap on each holdable resource that is itself an upgradeable object; a next advance priced above the current cap; a conversion rule for value arriving above the cap; and a separate holding area whose transfer into the capped store fills it without discarding the remainder.
**Why it is not covered.** Soft Currency lists capped-earning soft currency as a variant, which covers a limit on earning, not a structure in which raising the limit is itself a priced step on the progression path. No approved entry describes the overflow conversion or the way a cap inserts a mandatory intermediate purchase between the user and a stated goal.
**Recurrence elsewhere:** plausible. The structure is describable without reference to this product and needs only a stored resource, an upgradeable cap, and a cost above it.
**Caveat.** The conversion rate above the cap was never shown (O59), and only one direction of the pattern was seen in operation, where a town hall cost exceeded gold capacity (O54). Whether the cap is set deliberately below the next cost as a rule, or the two simply crossed once, cannot be settled from these transcripts.

### Advisory Progression Warning

**Draft definition.** At the moment a user commits to an advance they are entitled to make, the product interrupts to state that the advance will make the experience harder and recommends a different action first, while leaving the original action available.

**Source observations:** O30.
**Conditions it appears to depend on:** an advance the user has already paid for or qualified for; a consequence of that advance that the product judges adverse at the user's current state; an interruption placed at the commit point rather than earlier; a named alternative action; and an unchanged route to proceed anyway.
**Why it is not covered.** No approved entry describes the product arguing against a progression step. Contextual education, in the layer three patterns, covers teaching at the point of need; this is a recommendation against an action rather than an explanation of one. Leveling and Milestone classify the states involved, not the interruption.
**Recurrence elsewhere:** plausible. The structure applies wherever advancing raises difficulty faster than the user's capability, which is not particular to this product, though the transcripts contain no evidence about other products.
**Caveat.** One instance was observed (O30). A single occurrence establishes that the surface exists but not that it is a general behavior of the product, and the same warning was not seen at other town hall upgrades in the sessions.

### Parallel Second Track

**Draft definition.** Partway through a product's progression, a second self-contained instance of the core loop opens, with its own progression state, its own currencies, its own ranking ladder, and its own construction and research, connected to the first by a small number of shared items and by a shared premium balance.

**Source observations:** O45, O46, O53, O57.
**Conditions it appears to depend on:** an unlock placed some way into the first track; a duplicated but distinct loop with its own rules, here defense earning resources and a longer planning window; separate currencies not usable in the first track; a separate ranking ladder; a shared premium balance and a small set of items that work in both; and a stated obligation to maintain both.
**Why it is not covered.** No approved entry describes the duplication of a product's whole loop as a second parallel instance. Leveling classifies each track's states separately, and the entries for currencies classify each balance separately, but nothing classifies the relationship between the two instances or the demand it places on the user to maintain both.
**Recurrence elsewhere:** plausible. The pattern is describable as a second instance of any core loop with its own economy, and does not depend on this product's subject matter.
**Caveat.** The second track was played for part of one session only (O45). Whether it converges with the first track later, whether progress in one gates the other beyond the initial unlock, and what the shared items are beyond those observed were not established.

# Close: system view

The town hall level is the spine. Almost everything else in the product is either a condition on it, a consequence of it, or a way of reaching it sooner. Buildings, troops, spells, heroes, modes, cosmetics, decorations, leagues, and the pass itself are all gated on it (O39, O47), and every screen that offers the user a next action points back toward it (O28, O35). The cost curve is steep, from 4,000 gold at level 2 to a stated million at level 7 (O28), so the level sets the size of the resource problem the rest of the system exists to solve.

That problem is solved in three ways, and the product keeps all three open at once. Resources accumulate on their own in collectors (O20); they are taken from other players in raids (O23); and they are bought, indirectly, by converting purchased gems (O51). The three routes are not separate systems but one substitution: gems replace time, and time replaces raiding. The same substitution appears at every wait, where the gem price of ending a wait sits on the screen that announces it (O19), and at the builder slot, where the number of waits that can run at once is itself sold (O16).

Against accumulation sits loss. What the user holds is visible to other players and can be taken (O23, O70), and the countdown on the shield at the top of the screen states how long that is not true for (O89). The shield is sold in gems (O83), and being attacked grants a short one automatically (O90), so the loss condition is both the reason to come back and a reason to buy. The treasury, the piggy bank, and the storage caps all sit on the same axis: value held in some places is safe and value held in others is not, and moving value between them is a decision the user makes repeatedly (O54, O55, O85).

The waits are what structure the sessions. They are short enough during the guided opening to be invisible, and by town hall 6 they are twelve hours (O18), which is long enough to end a session and produce the completed-while-away summary on the next one (O88). Once the builders are occupied there is nothing left to do but attack, and once the attack is done there is nothing left to do but wait, which is where both recorded sessions after the first one ended (O95).

The social layer is late and gated. The clan castle must be rebuilt before any of it exists (O41), and the achievement list pushes toward it by making reinforcement requests a criterion (O33, O60). Once inside, the clan supplies things the solo loop does not: troops that cost the user nothing (O60), a war with a two-day rhythm of its own (O65), an event calendar that runs on multi-day cycles (O91), and a second shared construction project in the clan capital (O67). It also supplies the only observed gate that another person controls, since war participation depends on a leader choosing the user (O43).

Monetization is layered behind the same progression. The store opens on offers every time (O77), the offers carry countdowns (O94), and a new pack arrives at each town hall level (O79), so the offer set tracks the user's state rather than sitting still. The pass, the largest offer, is withheld entirely until town hall 7 (O47), which the narrator estimates at six or seven hours of play (O47). By the time it appears, the user holds a village, a second village, a clan, an account level, and a set of partially filled tracks, and the pass prices benefits against all of them at once: builders, research, clan castle requests, both villages' currencies, the clan capital, and a five times multiplier on a reserve that has been filling from battles the whole time (O84, O85).

The dependencies run in one direction almost everywhere. Storage capacity gates resources, resources gate the town hall, the town hall gates content, content gates the pass, and the pass prices the things the town hall made necessary. The two places where the direction reverses are the clan, where another person's decision gates the user (O43), and the advisory warning, where the product itself argues against the advance it has spent the whole session pushing toward (O30).
