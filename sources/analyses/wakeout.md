## Overview

Wakeout is an iOS app built around short movement breaks taken during the day. It measures movement as a unit it calls Wake Out Watts, accumulates those watts against a daily activity goal, and prompts the user to move through scheduled reminders. The transcripts render the app name variously as "Wake Out," "Wakeout," "Wake up," and "Waco"; this file uses Wakeout.

## First impression

The flow then moved to setup. A screen offered to assess the user's "sedentary index," stating that current habits help determine an activity plan. ## Core activity

The session shows an app organized around repeated one minute movement breaks and a running daily watts total. Inside the app the user starts a "move now, 1 minute" wakeout, which plays a video of an exercise with four bars below it, each bar a 15 second exercise with a start button and a countdown. A "get another move" button replaces the current exercise with a different one and restarts that bar's countdown. The narrator completed four exercises in one wakeout: arm shakes, shoulder back rolls, twist and flank arms, and full body tremors. On completion the app showed a pop up granting 50 Wake Out Watts, named the completed move "Tension Release Shakedown," and asked "do you feel better?" with three buttons: yes, a little, and no. The narrator recorded that the yes button was the largest and no the smallest, and gave two different estimates of the proportions in the same passage, so the exact sizes are ambiguous.

Outside the wakeout itself, the user browses exercise packs, favorites moves, runs a work timer, reviews a progress screen, and configures reminders and goals in settings. A navigation bar at the bottom of the home screen has five options: Now, Explore, Timer, Progress, and Settings.

## Mechanics observed

### Experience Points · Core · strongly supported

**What was observed:**

The app grants a quantified unit it calls Wake Out Watts for movement. Onboarding stated that all movement generates watts and listed weightlifting, walking, climbing stairs, standing, and stretching. Completing a wakeout produced a pop up reading "earned 50 wake out watts, you just completed Tension Release Shakedown," and the watts total moved from 613 to 663 out of 700 on the Now screen.

The accumulated total is measured against defined thresholds set by a difficulty setting. The difficulty screen, headed "adjust your daily activity goal to match your fitness level," offered very easy, easy, medium, hard, and very hard. Easy was the default and showed an active goal of 500 watts, a stretch goal of 200 watts, and max watts per hour of 150, with copy stating "we cap hourly watts to encourage movement throughout the day, not just one intense session." Very easy showed an active goal of 400 watts and a stretch goal of 100 watts; medium 700 and 300; hard 900 and 300 additional; very hard 1100 watts and a stretch goal of plus 400 watts.

Watts are also maintained as persistent totals. A watts stats section showed total watts earned 663, watts earned this week 663, most watts in a day 663 on that day, and most watts in a week this week. An activity log tab showed, hour by hour, how many watts were generated and what the activities were, listed as movement, stairs, exercising, and other. An advanced settings section showed "moving goal 500 watts, earn watts as you move toward the daily goal," on by default.

The transcript does not specify whether the daily watts total resets at a day boundary, and does not show any state other than the daily goal, the streak, and the stats that changes as watts accumulate.

**How it is presented:**

The current total appears at the top left of the Now screen as "goal 613 out of 700," above a bar that fills toward the total. The bar carries a check mark partway along, and the narrator recorded that progress continues past that check mark toward 700. Watts also appear in the completion pop up after a move, in the progress screen calendar per day, in the watts stats section, and in the activity log.

**Classification basis:**

The entry's invariant requires that the system adds quantified progression value after qualifying events and that the accumulated value contributes to reaching a defined threshold or advancing within a progression structure. Both are directly observed: 50 watts granted for a completed move, and a stated active goal of 500 watts with a stretch goal above it. The units accumulate across events, are maintained separately from the individual actions, and are observable through a balance, a bar, a grant message, and a threshold crossing. Exclusions were checked. The units are not exchangeable for any product-defined result anywhere in the session, so Spendable Credits and Tokens and Soft Currency do not apply. They are not consumed by activity, so Energy does not apply. They are not only a score or comparative result, since a defined threshold depends on them. The hourly cap matches the entry's capped variant, and the period-bounded reading of the daily goal is permitted by the entry's non-required conditions.

**What is notable:**

The pop up celebrating the achieved goal appeared on the first screen after purchase, before the user had seen or set any goal. The narrator recorded this directly: "they've been tracking my movement today, but I didn't set any active goal before that."

**Screenshot suggestions:**

- The Now screen top area showing "goal 613 out of 700," the streak, and the bar with its check mark. This shows the accumulated units, the threshold, and the fact that progress continues past the check mark.
- The completion pop up reading "earned 50 wake out watts, you just completed Tension Release Shakedown." This shows a quantified grant tied to a specific qualifying event.
- The difficulty screen showing very easy through very hard with active goal, stretch goal, and max watts per hour. This shows the defined thresholds the units accumulate toward.

## Behavioral interpretation

This section is separate from the observational record above. Every claim is a reading of the evidence, not an additional observation, and none of it is an effectiveness claim.

### Patterns instantiated

**Visible Progress** · confirmed. The watts bar converts accumulating movement into a representation that changes with activity, showing current position, a check-marked point, and remaining distance to 700. The trend graph and the hourly activity log add further representations of the same underlying measure.

**Immediate Feedback** · confirmed. Completing a wakeout produced a pop up granting 50 watts and a total that moved from 613 to 663 in the same moment. The four bars fill during the exercise itself.

**Milestone Recognition** · confirmed. Crossing the daily activity threshold produced a pop up, a check mark on the bar, and an increment to the recorded day-state counters, all distinct from the bar simply advancing.

**Endowed Head Start** · strongly supported. Onboarding requested Apple Health history with a choice of 30 days or full history, and the daily goal was already satisfied on the first screen after purchase from movement the user had not performed inside the app. The credited state reduced the remaining requirement for that day to zero. The alternative reading is that the app is simply reporting an accurate measurement of the day, which would make the credited progress a faithful record rather than a head start; what would separate them is whether the app credits pre-app history toward the current day's goal or only movement recorded after install.

**Continuity Stakes** · strongly supported. A one day streak state is maintained beside the goal, a longest streak is recorded, and the product's own copy attaches a days-off control to not breaking the streak. What is not observed is the differential response after a lapse, which is what the pattern's invariant ultimately rests on.

**Failure Recovery** · plausible. The take a day off control, with its "don't break your streak" copy, describes a route by which a missed period does not produce the ordinary consequence. The session shows the control and its copy but no lapse and no preserved state, so the counterfactual is stated by the product rather than observed.

**Earned Access** · strongly supported. Active Pass makes access to selected external apps conditional on completing a wakeout or on physical activity tracked by Apple Health. The entitlement structure is stated by the product and configured by the user; it was not observed operating.

**Low-Friction Entry** · strongly supported. The default wakeout is one minute long and composed of four 15 second exercises; the Now screen carries a single move now button; lock screen widgets offer four one-tap launches; difficulty defaults to easy; the three second demo and music toggles are on by default and auto start next is off by default; and a get another move button swaps an exercise without leaving the flow.

**Contextual Education** · confirmed. Explanatory screens are attached to specific decision points: the Apple Health description screen before the health prompt, the notification frequency and schedule screen before the notification prompt, the pre prompt screen that describes and scripts the Screen Time dialog, the info pop up that opens the Timer tab, and the widget instructions.

**Repetition Variation** · strongly supported. The get another move button substitutes a different exercise within the same wakeout, the Now screen surfaced evening-appropriate packs, and the catalog holds a large number of named packs across contexts. The paywall states 2000 plus exercises.

**Scheduled Return Triggers** · plausible. Reminders are bounded by configured active hours and selected days of the week, with a frequency band chosen by the user, and the app states it uses artificial intelligence to time them. The daily goal and streak give the current day a state that later days do not carry. The competing reading is that the reminders are notification delivery without a period-specific change in product value; what would separate them is observing whether the goal and its state reset at a day boundary.

Patterns checked and not classified: **Constrained Capacity**, because the 150 watts per hour cap limits how much of the measure can be earned in an hour rather than bounding whether the activity can be performed.

### Obstacles the design appears to address

These are hypotheses about the product's apparent aims, read from the observed design. They are not claims about users.

**Starting Cost Is Too High** · strongly supported. The one minute default, the 15 second exercise units, the single move now button, the lock screen quick launches, and the easy default difficulty all reduce what is required before the first movement occurs. The onboarding copy states the thesis directly: it is more important to move often than to exercise. Alternative reading: **Effort Feels Disproportionate to Payoff**, where the design is aimed at the exchange rather than at initiation; what would separate them is whether the short format is presented as easier to begin or as better value for the time spent.

**Value Is Not Understood** · strongly supported. Roughly the first half of onboarding is an argument that sedentary time is harmful and that exercise does not offset it, delivered through quotes, a consequences list, and illustrations before any product function appears. The contextual pre prompts perform the same function at each permission. Alternative reading: **Spend Reluctance**, since the same argument terminates in a paywall that cannot be dismissed; what would separate them is whether comparable education appears at points with no purchase decision attached.

**Habit Not Yet Formed** · plausible. Scheduled reminders inside configured active hours, on selected days, timed by a stated algorithm, address a behavior that must recur without the user initiating it. Alternative reading: **No Reason to Return Today**, addressed by the daily goal and streak rather than by cueing. The evidence does not separate them; both readings fit the same reminder and daily-goal structure.

**One Failure Breaks the Pattern** · plausible. The take a day off control exists specifically to hold the streak across a period when the user does not participate. Alternative reading: it is a notification mute whose streak language is copy rather than behavior; what would separate them is observing the streak state across a taken day off.

**Spend Reluctance** · plausible. The paywall carries four price points, a comparison table against the cost of continuing to sit, seven testimonials structured as before and after, an editor's choice and app of the year set of tags, and a discounted gift alternative. Alternative reading: these are ordinary conversion surfaces present regardless of any diagnosed hesitation.

### Principles

Calibrated language only. Product evidence can establish the decision structure; it cannot establish that any user responded through it.

The app creates conditions consistent with **Endowed Progress**: the current goal instance began already satisfied, from history the user did not generate inside the app. · strongly supported.

The app creates conditions consistent with **Goal Gradient**: remaining distance to the threshold is displayed numerically and as a bar, and continues past the check mark toward 700. · plausible. An alternative is simple measurement display with no distance-to-endpoint function.

The app creates conditions consistent with **Loss Aversion**: a streak state is displayed and the product's own copy frames a control around not breaking it. · plausible. Alternatives include Commitment and Consistency, since the user configures active hours, days, difficulty, and reminder frequency, and Sunk-Cost Sensitivity, neither of which the evidence separates.

The app creates conditions consistent with **Default and Friction Sensitivity**: difficulty defaults to easy, the three second demo and music toggles are on and auto start next is off by default, and the Screen Time pre prompt tells the user which button to press. · strongly supported as structure.

The app creates conditions consistent with **Social Proof** at the purchase decision: the paywall shows editor's choice, app of the year, 10,000 five star reviews, "join thousands who found an easier way to stay active," and seven testimonials; the widget screen states that users who add the widget stay more on top of their day. · plausible.

The app creates conditions consistent with **Autonomy Need**: notification frequency bands and off, active hours and days, five difficulty levels, a set of selectable exercise durations, eleven notification personas, editable focus activities, and a days off control. · plausible. An alternative is preference matching and configuration depth without an agency function.

No claim is made anywhere in this section that any of these mechanics or patterns produced a behavioral result.

## Session rhythm

A wakeout is one minute by default, composed of four 15 second exercises, with further durations selectable on a move profile, listed in a passage the transcript garbles beyond 30 seconds, 1 minute, 5 minutes and 10 minutes. The Healthy Work Timer runs 25 minute focused sprints by default, with the focus time adjustable and a session started at 30 minutes in the observed run.

Return pressure comes from reminders bounded by an activity schedule set by default to 8 in the morning until 10 in the evening on Monday through Friday, at a frequency the user selects from four bands, with the app stating it times them using artificial intelligence. Home screen and lock screen widgets are offered as further re-entry points, and Active Pass is described as pausing selected apps until movement occurs.

The session-end moment observed was the completion screen after a wakeout, which showed the granted watts, the updated total, the completed move name, add to favorites, share move, a move again section listing favorites, and the gift banner at the bottom. The transcript does not show a day boundary, a reset, or a return on a following day. The narrator ended by stating the intention to try it again the next day.
