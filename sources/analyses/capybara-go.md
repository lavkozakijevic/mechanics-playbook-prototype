**Source.** This analysis is built from three Granola walkthrough sessions recorded by a single narrator and retrieved as full transcripts: "Capybara Go! I" (12 May 2026), "Capybara Go! II" (13 May 2026) and "Capybara Go! III" (15 May 2026). Session I covers first launch, the first run through its defeat on day 40, the first home screen, talents, the store and equipment. Session II reopens the app more than 24 hours later and covers energy, the sidebar, rewarded ads and a second run that reaches day 59. Session III begins after Chapter 1 has been cleared and covers mail, Growth Fund, Privilege Card, the Seven-Day Carnival, Tower Challenge, pets, raids, tasks, the sign-in event, Timed Growth events and the Black Market, ending in Chapter 3. The transcripts are machine speech-to-text of live narration; names and numbers are reported as transcribed, and passages where the narration is fragmented are marked at the relevant observation. The sessions contain no typed notes. The app version is reported as spoken in Session I.

## 1. Onboarding and first run

**O1. Launch permission prompts and asset download.**

**Observed:** On first launch, Capybara Go! shows a system prompt asking to send notifications while the loading screen is displayed (tier: directly observed). While the app checks for updates, a second prompt asks permission to track activity across other companies' apps and websites (tier: directly observed). The app then downloads assets, shows a login step and opens gameplay (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The asset download is stated as 86 megabytes and takes noticeable time (tier: directly observed).
- The version is spoken as "1812" at the login step (tier: directly observed).
- The loading screen artwork carries Lunar New Year designs (tier: directly observed).
- Both permission prompts appear before any gameplay (tier: directly observed).

## 2. Core loop and automation

**O8. Day-by-day run advanced by tapping.**

**Observed:** A run is a sequence of numbered days, and the user taps a button to advance to the next day, which resolves an event with a text message and an outcome (tier: directly observed). Outcomes include stat increases, stat decreases, experience, gold, health changes and battles (tier: directly observed). Before a battle, the advance button changes to "Battle" (tier: directly observed). There is no autoplay for advancing days (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Run one, days 2 to 7: an abandoned chest with a strength potion, attack up 7%, labelled "minor fortune"; a caterpillar princess, 20 experience; escaping a stalking beast, 10 experience; a damaged longsword, attack down 5%; a "big win" animation with coins, then 100 experience and 100 gold; "bad luck", a music box that lowers max health (tier: directly observed).
- Events carry luck framing such as "minor fortune", "big win", "minor win" and "bad luck" (tier: directly observed).
- Run two opens with a different event order from run one, including a goblin warrior exchange, a legendary drake sighting, a mountain climb and insect bites before the first necromancer (tier: directly observed).
- Some event texts recur across and within runs, including the necromancy aura before a necromancer battle, the campsite, the weak slimes and the eerie skeleton (tier: directly observed).
- The rule that selects each day's event is not stated (tier: unresolved).

*Cross-reference: O9, O12, O13, O14, O21.*

**O9. Automatic battles.**

**Observed:** Battles run without user input, and the narrator states "We're doing nothing" while the character and enemy exchange hits over numbered rounds (tier: directly observed). Victory grants experience and gold (tier: directly observed). The skills in use appear at the top of the battle screen (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**

- Enemy strength rises through a run: a later goblin has much more health than earlier enemies, and a later necromancer looks stronger than the first (tier: directly observed).
- At 19% health the heart indicator flashes (tier: directly observed).
- One battle ended immediately with an instant kill (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O10, O15, O18.*

# Pass Two: tagging

## Applied tags

**Tag:** Challenge

**Observations:** O21, O22

**Supporting observations:** O16, O23, O44

**Confidence:** directly observed

**Role:** engagement

The chapter day target in O21 and the floor attempts in O22 organize what the user does inside a session around a success condition that can fail.

**Rationale:** Each chapter run and each Tower floor is a bounded objective instance rather than an open target, a recommendation or a ranking, so no exclusion condition applies (tier: directly observed). A chapter run has a success condition, surviving 60 days in Chapter 1, and ends at defeat, and the defeat summary in O16 differs from the clear, which grants gold and moves the capybara to the next area (tier: directly observed). A Tower floor has a success condition, defeating the floor's enemies, an attempt boundary of one ticket, and distinct victory and defeat responses (tier: directly observed).

**Variants present:** Attempt-bounded challenge (Tower floors, one ticket per attempt); session challenge (a chapter run ends within the run); threshold challenge (surviving 60 days); multi-stage challenge (Tower of Explorers floors 1 to 10 with a clear reward); automatically assigned challenge; rewarded challenge.

**Variants not established:** Time-boxed challenge; head-to-head challenge; group challenge; opt-in challenge; recurring challenge; adaptive challenge. Raids in O23 require tickets, but their success condition was not narrated, and the carnival tasks in O44 showed no completion state.

**Alternative considered:** Leveling for chapters. The home screen shows the current area, but chapters are ordered content units with their own success conditions, and the Leveling entry excludes numbered stages that are content units when no separate current level state is maintained (tier: plausible). A chapter number presented as the account's progression state apart from the map would separate the two.

**Tag:** Companion

**Observations:** O29, O30

**Supporting observations:** O23, O45, O46, O47, O62

**Confidence:** directly observed

**Role:** engagement, monetization

Pets act in the battles and raids that make up play in O23 and O29, and their acquisition and development are priced in gems, paid for with ad views and sold in packs and event offers in O29, O45 and O53.

**Rationale:** Pets are entities distinct from the capybara, held in a pet list between runs, and they act in the activity rather than modifying the character from an equipment slot, so the equipment exclusion does not apply (tier: directly observed). The pets screen maintains three battle positions, one open and two locked, and a hatched pet is swiped into the open position (tier: directly observed). Pets attack in battle and alongside the character in raid battles (tier: directly observed). Each pet holds a level, battle skills that unlock at higher levels, and an enhancement rule requiring further copies of the same pet together with pet food (tier: directly observed). The acquisition routes seen are egg hatches for three ads or for 300 or 600 gems (O29), the Growth Shop's ticket exchange (O47) and sign-in rewards of five Snowy pets and the mythic pet Flash (O62) (tier: directly observed). The size of a pet's contribution to a battle outcome was not shown (tier: unresolved). The stated condition for the next position, level 4, does not name which level it refers to (tier: unresolved).

# Pass Three: proposed new tags

P1 and P4 were approved and written into `/library/approved` on 11 Sep 2026. Five further proposals were rejected on scope and moved to "Entries considered and not applied" above.

### P1. Companion Units

**Draft definition.** A companion unit is a persistent secondary entity, separate from the user's primary character, that the product lets the user acquire, assign to a limited number of active positions and develop over time, and that acts alongside the primary character within the core activity.

**Source observations:** O23, O29, O30, O47

**Conditions it appears to depend on:** A route for acquiring units, here egg hatches and a ticket shop (O29, O47); a limited number of active positions that open with progress (O29); participation of the unit in the core activity, here battles and raids (O23, O29); progression that belongs to the unit, here levels, battle skills, duplicate copies and pet food (O30).

**Why it is not covered.** Leveling describes the pet level state and Loot Box and Variable Reward Outcome describe how eggs resolve, but no entry describes a secondary entity that is slotted, developed and active in the core activity as a unit. Set Collection requires member-level completeness tracking, which was not shown for pets. Cosmetic Customization is excluded because pets act in battle.

**Recurrence elsewhere:** The structure would recur in any product that lets users hold helpers, pets or secondary characters that take part in its main activity and grow separately from the user's own progression.

**Caveat.** The size of a pet's contribution in battle was not shown, and the "level 4" condition for the next slot does not name what level it refers to (O29).

**Status:** Approved and written into the library as the entry Companion on 11 Sep 2026. The caveat is carried in that entry as an open separating-evidence note.

### P4. Progression Fund

**Draft definition.** A progression fund is a reward track with no end date whose positions are reached by advancing a progression measure that already exists elsewhere in the product, with a free lane and a purchasable lane that pays larger rewards at the same positions.

**Source observations:** O58

**Conditions it appears to depend on:** An existing measure to attach to, here talent level, Tower Challenge floors, Dungeon Dive floors, hard mode progress, guru tickets used and mythic treasures collected (O58); ordered reward positions; a free lane and a purchasable lane; no end timer.

**Why it is not covered.** Seasonal Progression Pass requires a seasonal boundary and excludes ordered reward tracks without one. Milestone recognizes individual thresholds but not a parallel track across the same measure. Monthly Reward Card releases rewards by time interval rather than by progress.

**Recurrence elsewhere:** The structure would recur wherever a product attaches a reward track to progress it already measures and offers a larger lane for purchase.

**Caveat.** The designed experience rests on the free lane and its link to progress. Whether free rewards can be claimed without buying the fund was not shown (O58); if they cannot, the surface is a purchase offer and fails the commerce test. No fund was seen advancing.

**Status:** Approved and written into the library as the entry Progression Fund on 11 Sep 2026. The caveat is carried in that entry as an open separating-evidence note.

# Close: system view

**Energy gates the core loop.** Starting a run spends energy (O33), and energy returns through regeneration, ads, gems, free daily packs and run end grants (O16, O34), with overflow held in mail for later claim (O35) (tier: directly observed). Runs are chapter attempts built from day events, automatic battles and in-run skill choices (O8, O9, O10, O21), and every run, won or lost, pays out gold (O16, O38) (tier: directly observed).

**Gold turns runs into permanent strength.** Gold buys talent levels (O24), which advance the talent progress bar and its rewards (O25) and the rank title tree (O26), and those gains raise the starting stats of the next run (O17) (tier: strongly supported). Skills chosen inside a run do not carry over (O17), so the permanent layer is what persists from one attempt to the next (tier: strongly supported). Deeper runs clear chapters (O21), and chapter clears and survival-day thresholds are the keys to nearly every other feature (O3, O37) (tier: directly observed).

**Each unlock adds a loop with its own inputs.** Tower Challenge consumes tickets (O22, O36) and pays power stones and keys (O40), which feed equipment enhancement (O27) and key-opened chests (O41); losing a floor routes the user back to talents and equipment (O31) (tier: directly observed). Equipment arrives through crates and chests paid with gems, keys or ads (O41, O42), and identical items merge upward (O28) (tier: directly observed). Pets arrive through egg hatches paid with gems or ads (O29), grow with duplicates and pet food (O30), take part in raids that pay pet food (O23), and are the subject of the Pet Auspice event, which rewards egg openings and sells eggs (O45) (tier: directly observed).

**Ads are an alternative price across the loops.** Energy, crate opens, egg hatches, dragon tickets and Black Market gold can each be obtained by watching ads (O56), and every ad prompt carries the Ad-Free Card offer (O56, O59) (tier: directly observed).

**The return layer sits on top of the loops.** The sign-in event (O62), free daily claims (O53, O59), daily and weekly tasks (O43), the Seven-Day Carnival (O44), the Battle Pass (O57) and Timed Growth events (O45) each attach rewards to activity within a day, a week or an event window, and countdowns on shops, crates, chests and events mark those windows (O63, O64) (tier: directly observed). Task content points back into the loops, asking for Black Market purchases, chest openings and raids (O43), and carnival tasks ask for Tower progress and equipment draws (O44) (tier: directly observed).

**Purchases attach to each measure.** Gems are the common currency across refills, draws and hatches and are sold directly with a first top-up bonus (O39, O54) (tier: directly observed). Funds attach a purchasable lane to talent level, Tower floors, Dungeon Dive, hard mode and main quests (O58), cards sell daily collection (O59), packs refresh daily, weekly and monthly with value labels (O52, O53, O55), and offers appear at a chapter clear, at day 59 of a run and when gold runs short (O60, O61) (tier: directly observed).

**Onboarding sequences all of this.** The app gives no explanation before a first run of almost 20 minutes (O2) and then introduces talents, the shop, equipment and later features one pop-up at a time with pointer guidance (O3), while one lock message contradicted the shop unlock that followed it (O5) (tier: directly observed).
