# Pass two: tagging

## Applied tags

**Tag:** Challenge

**Observations:** O24, O25, O27

**Supporting observations:** O26, O32

**Confidence:** directly observed

**Role:** engagement, social

Each challenge is a bounded objective the user enters and works toward inside a session (O24, O25), and entries are submitted, viewed and voted on among other identified people (O25, O26, O27).

**Rationale:** Picsart presents discrete challenge instances, each with a name, a type, rules, a countdown to the minute, and a stated prize (O24, O25). The success condition is winning, expressed as a prize of credits or a place in the winners' gallery, and concluded instances persist in a history listing winners from first to tenth place (O24, O27). The boundary is the countdown, and the product distinguishes winners from other entrants in that history (O25, O27). A separate voting stage with its own time limit sits between entry and history (O26). Space owners can also create challenges within their spaces (O32). Under the presence standard the implementation is recognizable from the product's own framing and from its concluded states. How winners are chosen, what happens after a submission, whether prizes are delivered automatically, and how a space challenge is bounded were not observed and are recorded as not observed.

**Variants present:** Time-boxed challenge, opt-in challenge, head-to-head challenge, rewarded challenge.

**Variants not established:** Recurring challenge, event-bounded challenge, multi-stage challenge, adaptive challenge, group challenge, community challenge.

**Alternative considered:** A contest in which the product only ranks submissions without defining a challenge-level success condition, which the entry excludes. The evidence against it is that each instance states in advance a reward tied to winning and a deadline for entry (O24, O25); the evidence that would settle the success condition fully is a session carried through a challenge's close, showing how winners are determined and what non-winning entrants receive.

**Tag:** Leaderboard

**Observations:** O27

**Supporting observations:** O24, O26

**Confidence:** strongly supported

**Role:** social

The ordered list sets creators' entries against one another by name and image, and the winners are other identified people (O27).

**Rationale:** Challenge history presents, for each concluded challenge, winners in ordered positions from first to tenth (O27). The compared entities are entrants in the same challenge, the scope is the challenge, and ten positions are shown in relative order in one view (O27). The entry requires a defined comparison measure. The measure is not stated on screen; the entry stage, a separate voting stage showing rewards, and a history of placed winners point to votes as the measure, but editorial selection informed by votes would fit the same screens (O24, O26, O27). The ordering and scope are directly observed, and the measure is plausible, so the tag is held at strongly supported rather than directly observed. Vote counts are not shown while voting (O26).

**Variants present:** Individual leaderboard, event leaderboard, final leaderboard, truncated leaderboard.

**Variants not established:** Rewarded leaderboard, live leaderboard, periodic leaderboard, anonymous leaderboard.

The evidence against it is the explicit first-to-tenth placement labels (O27); the evidence that would settle the measure is a screen or rule stating how placement is decided, or a concluded challenge showing vote totals beside positions. # Pass three: proposed new tags

One proposal remains open. A second, Try Before Apply, was proposed from this analysis and rejected on scope on 16 Sep 2026; it is recorded in Entries considered and not applied.

### Try This Look

**Draft definition.** A reuse prompt attaches to a creation attributed to another actor, shown in a browsing surface, a control that applies the same effect, template, tool or generation settings to the viewer's own material, and presents the creation with what produced it, such as a before and after pair or the prompt used. The subject of the control is the method behind someone else's creation, and its destination is the viewer's own creating.

**Source observations:** O30, O31

**Conditions it appears to depend on:** A feed or gallery of creations attributed to other actors; a control on the individual item rather than on the surface; disclosure of how the item was made, as a before and after view or a visible prompt; and a route from that control into the product's creation tools applied to the viewer's material.

**Why it is not covered.** Social Feed classifies the stream of socially attributed items but not a per-item route that converts viewing into the viewer's own creation. Shareable Win covers representations of accomplishments sent outward, and excludes the user's own work. Community Space and Group Membership cover shared contexts rather than reuse of another creator's method. No approved entry classifies the conversion of browsing someone else's output into a start on one's own.

**Recurrence elsewhere:** Expected in creative, design and short-video products, where a control on another person's post applies the same effect, template or settings to the viewer's own material (tier: plausible, not yet observed in a second analyzed app). No second app carrying this shape has been recorded in this library.

**Caveat.** Held as an open proposal on 16 Sep 2026. The Try control was never tapped and the AI Vibe prompt was never run (O30, O31). What is unobserved is the substance of the mechanic rather than a detail around it: where the control leads, whether the viewer's own material is loaded into it, and whether reuse costs credits or requires Pro. Two pieces of evidence would settle the proposal. The first is a Picsart session that taps Try on a feed item and follows it through to a finished result, recording the screen it opens, what material is loaded, and any credit deduction or paywall met on the way. The second is a walkthrough of another app carrying the same shape: a control on another person's creation that applies the same method to the viewer's own material. Until both exist, the proposal stays open and is not written as an entry.

# Close: system view

Picsart runs an editing loop and routes most of its other surfaces either into that loop or toward the Pro and Plus subscriptions. The first paywall arrives before any tool is seen, with only Pro offered and a close control that is hard to find, and the onboarding questions follow only once it is closed (O4, O5, O6, O7). Inside the app, the Create surface and the editor are the loop (O9, O10, O11, O12), and the free experience of that loop is bounded at two kinds of point: Pro markers on individual tools, where Remove requests payment at Apply (O18), and premium AI models, where Generate requests payment with no preview (O19). Both requests open the same Pro and Plus offers that the Try Pro button opens from the home screen (O37).

Credits connect the AI half of the loop to the economy and the economy to the subscriptions. A free profile holds a weekly allowance of five credits priced against a long list of AI tools (O20, O21). The only observed route to more is a subscription, stated as up to 200 or 500 credits on the paid plans, and the Generate paywall sells the upgrade in terms of credits rather than tools (O22, O38, O39). The same credits appear as challenge prizes, which ties the community side of the product to the balance the editor draws on (O24, O25). Whether a prize or a generation actually changes the balance was not observed, so this link is stated on the product's own copy.

The community side has three parts that feed one another. Challenges take entries, open a separate voting stage, and keep a history of winners placed first to tenth (O24, O25, O26, O27). Submissions and winners link to creator profiles, where following is a single tap (O26, O28), and followed accounts' activity reaches the notification list (O41). Spaces add owner-run challenges and join and leave controls beside Picsart's own challenges (O32). The Discover feed returns the user from browsing to creating: every item carries a Try control showing a before and after, and AI Vibe items disclose their prompts (O30, O31), which is the point at which other people's work re-enters the editing loop.

Outward reach is carried by invitations without a stated reward, per-project sharing with named people, export destinations, and a promotion for a separate drawing app (O33, O34, O35, O36). Return triggers were thin in this session: an in-app push prompt raised during export and a system notification prompt at the bell (O40, O41). No scheduled reward, streak or countdown-based reminder was observed; the challenge countdowns and the six-day credit renewal are the only time-based states seen, and the session did not show the product using either to prompt a return (O20, O25).
