---

## Overview

PicsArt is a photo and video editing app with a large embedded social platform. It combines a creation suite (AI editing tools, templates, drawing, collage, print) with a creator community (feeds, spaces, challenges, following). It operates on a three-tier model: Free (5 AI credits/week, 100 MB cloud, 2 devices), Plus ($64.99/year, 200 credits/week, 5 GB cloud, 5 devices), and Pro ($83.99/year, 500 credits/week, 100 GB cloud, 10 devices). The session covered onboarding through all five navigation tabs and settings.

---

## First impression

The app's loading screen shows "Editor's Choice." The first interactive element is Apple's tracking permission dialog. Then a sign-in carousel (six options: Apple, Google, Facebook, Snapchat, email, username) with a "join over 150 million creators worldwide" tagline. After selecting Apple sign-in, a paywall appears immediately — before onboarding completes and before any app content is shown. The paywall's close button is an X hidden inside a red image element in the top-left corner, described as "very hard actually to notice that it's there."

After dismissing the paywall, onboarding asks three questions: comfort level with editing apps (I'm new / basics / know what I'm doing), purpose (own business, side hustle, fun, artistic expression, hobby, school/uni), and content type (memes, video editing, scrapbooking, collages, photo touch-ups, selfie editing, school project, fan art, social media content). A final attribution question ("where did you hear about us?") completes onboarding.

---

## Core activity

Users create and edit photos and videos using AI-powered tools, templates, and manual editing, then share creations to their profile and feed. Between creative sessions, users browse an endless content feed, join and participate in community spaces, enter photo challenges, vote on challenge submissions, and follow other creators. The AI tool suite (image generation, background removal, object removal, video generation, etc.) is the primary differentiator from standard photo editors.

## Mechanics observed

### Challenges (`challenges`) · Core

**What was observed:**
Challenges are the central community engagement mechanic. They are accessible via the cup icon in the bottom navigation bar, with three sub-tabs: Active Challenges, Start Voting, and Challenge History.

**Active Challenges observed:**
- Bring in the Animals Challenge: 4 days remaining, 50 credits prize
- Leaf and Lady: active, prize details not fully described
- My Favorite View: 4 days 12 hours 38 minutes remaining, prize: "appear in the winner's gallery"

Each challenge has: a name, countdown timer, prize description, rules (submit up to 3 original photos, keep it natural, only share what you've created, respect the community), an About section, and a scrollable submissions gallery. The session observed 92 submissions for My Favorite View at time of viewing.

**Space challenges:** Beyond platform-wide challenges, individual community spaces can create their own challenges. "Each space that exists out there has its own challenge." Space challenges appear in a stories-style scrolling format when browsing spaces.

**Voting tab:** Active submissions from a challenge can be voted on by any user. Voting is possible for multiple entries simultaneously. Vote counts are not visible to voters ("we cannot see any information about how many people have voted so far"). The challenge history tab shows past results by place (1st through 10th) with profile links to winners.

**How it is presented:**
The challenges tab uses the cup icon in the main navigation — front-loaded alongside creation and discovery. The active challenge count (3 simultaneously) and the voting tab are visible without any prerequisite. Winners are featured in a named "winner's gallery," creating a display incentive beyond the credit prize.

**What is notable:**
Two types of challenge rewards were observed: credits (50 credits for Bring in the Animals) and a non-monetary gallery feature (My Favorite View). The gallery feature is a social recognition reward rather than a currency reward, making participation motivating even for users who would not spend credits. The space-level challenge system means challenges operate at two scales simultaneously — platform-wide and community-specific.

### Social Feed (`social-feed`) · Core

**What was observed:**
The Discover tab contains a multi-section content feed:

**For You feed:** An endless personalized feed of images and videos created by other users. Each post shows the creator's profile, creation, heart count, and comment count. Below each item is a "Try" button — tapping it opens the same editing tool that created that effect, allowing the viewer to apply it to their own photo. This "try from feed" mechanic directly converts content discovery into tool usage.

**Explore section:** Curated content organized by type — popular categories, AI Vibe content (AI-generated), creator spotlights, templates by format (Instagram story, business card, etc.).

**Spaces feed:** Community spaces each have their own content feed visible within the space. Following a creator and joining a space are both surfaces through which content enters the main feed.

**Creator profiles:** Each creator has a profile showing posts, stickers created, follower count, following count, and collections. Profiles are accessible from the feed, from challenge submissions, and from space member lists. Follow/unfollow is a single tap on any profile view.

**How it is presented:**
The Discover tab is accessible from the bottom navigation bar. The For You feed is the default landing view within Discover. The "Try" button appears beneath every item in the feed, consistently linking consumption to creation.

**What is notable:**
The "Try" button is the most direct consumption-to-creation conversion mechanic observed in the library. Unlike standard social feeds where viewing and creating are separate actions, PicsArt embeds a creation trigger directly into every feed post. A user scrolling the feed can start a new project from any post without navigating away.

### Community Groups (`community-groups`) · Supporting

**What was observed:**
Spaces are named communities within PicsArt, each with its own content feed, challenge system, rules, bookmarked tools, and member directory. Spaces are described as having "space owners" who can create challenges within the space.

**Observed space features:**
- Space feed: member-created content visible to all space members
- Space challenges: owner-created challenges specific to the space, viewable in a stories-style horizontal scroll
- Space rules: explicit community guidelines displayed on the space page
- Bookmarked tools: specific editing tools recommended or required by the space (not described in detail)
- Join/leave controls: single-tap joining; the member count and space owner are visible

**Discovery:** Spaces are browsable in the Explore section. The session noted "a great number of spaces present" without specifying counts.

**How it is presented:**
Spaces are in the Explore section of the Discover tab, not in primary navigation. Joining a space adds its content to the broader feed. Space challenges appear as a separate layer alongside platform-wide challenges.

**What is notable:**
The dual challenge system (platform-wide and space-specific) means active creators may simultaneously participate in a platform challenge and multiple space challenges. Space owners can set their own challenge rules and rewards, creating a user-generated challenge infrastructure rather than a purely platform-administered one.

### Achievements (`achievements`) · Shallow

**What was observed:**
The user profile shows a completion percentage ("50% complete") but does not specify what actions are required to reach 100%. The transcript noted: "it just says 50% complete but probably we need to upload the photo to make it complete." No named achievement badges, milestone markers, or explicit completion criteria were described.

The challenge history tab shows ranked results (1st through 10th place) for past challenges, functioning as a permanent record of competitive performance. Winners are linked and browsable — a form of persistent achievement display for top performers.

**How it is presented:**
Profile completion percentage is visible on the profile/settings screen. Challenge winners are in the Challenge History tab.

**What is notable:**
The profile completion percentage without a description of what needs to be done is a UX gap — it creates awareness of incompleteness without providing a clear path to resolution.

### Cosmetics (`cosmetics`) · Shallow

**What was observed:**
The AI Vibe content in the Explore section allows users to view, like, save to collections, and edit AI-generated images using prompts. Collections are described as public or private groupings of saved content — functioning as a curation and display layer on the profile. Users can create stickers that appear on their profile alongside posts.

No avatar customization, profile frame, or in-app visual identity system was described beyond the basic profile photo and username.

**How it is presented:**
Collections are created from the folder tab in navigation. Stickers are created from the creation suite. AI Vibe content is curated in the Explore section.

**What is notable:**
The combination of collections and stickers on a public profile provides a lightweight portfolio-style display system, but it operates through creative output rather than purchasable cosmetic items. This is closer to a portfolio mechanic than a cosmetics mechanic as defined in the library.

## How mechanics connect

**Challenges and social feed are mutually reinforcing.** Participating in a challenge produces content that enters the feed. Browsing the feed exposes users to challenge submissions from other creators, which encourages entry. Voting on challenge submissions is itself a feed browsing activity. The challenge system is therefore a structured content creation prompt that feeds the social platform's content supply.

**The "Try" button connects feed consumption to tool usage.** Every feed post has a Try button that opens the editing tool that created the visible effect. This connects discovery (browsing) to creation (using the tool) to potentially new content (sharing the result) in a single interaction chain. It also serves as a product demonstration: the feed is a gallery of what each tool can produce.

**Spaces extend challenges to a community level.** Platform challenges are broad (any user, any style). Space challenges are narrow (specific community, specific style or topic). A user who joins multiple spaces participates in multiple parallel challenge streams simultaneously, all feeding back into their public profile and the broader feed.

**Credits connect AI tool usage to monetization.** The free tier's 5 credits/week gate is the primary monetization lever — any use of AI generation tools beyond 5/week requires upgrading. The credit cost table (1 credit for AI sticker, 2 for logo/image generator, 3 for AI background, 4 for avatar, 50 for AI video) creates a hierarchy of tool access, with the most compelling AI outputs (video, avatar) requiring the most credits and therefore the strongest upgrade incentive.

## Session rhythm

PicsArt is a creation-on-demand tool used when there is content to create or edit. Session rhythm is project-driven rather than daily-incentive-driven.

**Return incentives observed:** Weekly credit refresh (free tier: 5 credits/week, renews in 6 days at time of session), active challenge deadlines (4-day countdowns visible on challenges tab), voting windows on active challenges, space challenge activity from joined spaces.

**Session triggers:** Creative project needs, challenge deadlines, browsing the For You feed.

**No daily return mechanic was observed.** The weekly credit reset and challenge countdowns create weekly rather than daily return pressure.

## What stood out

1. **The paywall appears before onboarding is complete, and the close button is deliberately camouflaged.** The paywall appears immediately after sign-in, before any app content is shown. The X button to dismiss it is "red, so you don't really notice it very easily" and positioned inside a decorative image element in the top-left corner. The session described it as "very hard actually to notice that it's there." This is among the most aggressive paywall presentation designs observed in the library — not through pricing, but through concealing the exit path.

2. **The free trial toggle is off by default and requires manual activation.** On the annual plan, a 3-day free trial is available — but the toggle to activate it is off by default. Without noticing and enabling it, the user commits to an immediate charge. The button label changes from "Continue" to "Continue with 3-day free trial" only when the toggle is enabled. The transcript noted: "free trial is not available with weekly, but it is available with yearly option and it is not toggled on by default — you have to tap on it to turn it on."

3. **The Plus plan ($64.99/year) only appears after the paywall is dismissed, not at first presentation.** On first sign-in, only the Pro plan ($83.99/year) is shown. After navigating into the app and tapping "Try Pro" again, both Pro and a cheaper Plus option appear. New users who dismiss the first paywall without noticing the Plus option may not realize a cheaper subscription exists. The session noted: "this is not an option that we got when we were logging in for the first time."

4. **The "Try" button on every feed post converts content browsing into tool usage in a single tap.** This is the most direct consumption-to-creation conversion mechanic in the library. Every post in the For You feed has a Try button that opens the specific editing tool that created that visible effect. A user who sees an appealing AI-generated background can immediately start creating their own with one tap, without navigating to the tool catalogue.

## Unrecognized mechanics

### `ai-credits` (Generative AI consumption token)
**What was observed:** AI credits are a weekly-refreshing consumption token used to access generative AI tools. Free tier: 5 credits/week. Plus: 200 credits/week. Pro: 500 credits/week. Credits cannot be purchased individually — only through upgrading the subscription tier. Each AI tool costs 1–50 credits per generation. Add-on credits (described in the credits detail screen as "do not expire") are mentioned but no purchase path for them was described in the session. The credit system is disclosed through the profile section rather than surfaced during onboarding or at the point of first AI tool use.
**Why it does not map to an existing mechanic:** Not `energy-lives` — credits do not regenerate over time; they refresh weekly as a batch. Not `hard-currency` — credits cannot be purchased directly with real money (only through subscription). Not `soft-currency` — credits are not earned through gameplay or activity; they are allocated by subscription tier. AI generation credits are a metered access token: a fixed weekly allocation that gates AI tool usage, with the allocation amount determined by subscription level rather than in-game behavior. This is a distinct pattern from both energy systems and currency systems.
**Worth adding to library:** Yes — metered AI credit systems (fixed periodic allocation, consumption by tool use, no individual purchase path) are appearing across AI-integrated apps (PicsArt, Canva, Perplexity) and represent a new category of access token distinct from traditional game currencies and energy systems.

### `try-from-feed`
**What was observed:** Every post in the PicsArt For You feed has a "Try" button beneath it. Tapping Try opens the specific editing tool, filter, or AI feature that was used to create the visible effect in that post, ready for the user to apply to their own photo. The session described "before and after" preview toggles visible on some feed posts alongside the Try button. This mechanic directly connects content consumption (browsing the feed) to tool usage (applying the same effect) in a single interaction.
**Why it does not map to an existing mechanic:** Not `social-feed` — the Try button is a creation-entry mechanism embedded in the feed, not a feed display feature. Not `challenges` — it does not involve a structured competitive objective. Try-from-feed is a discovery-to-creation conversion mechanic: a single button that converts passive browsing into active tool engagement, using the feed's own content as real-time product demonstrations.
**Worth adding to library:** Possibly — the pattern of embedding a creation entry point directly in a content feed (rather than requiring navigation to a separate creation section) appears in creative platform apps and is worth naming as a distinct UX/engagement mechanic.

### `creator-community-spaces`
**What was observed:** Spaces are named user-created communities within PicsArt, each with its own feed, challenge system, space owner, rules, and bookmarked tools. Spaces are browsable in the Explore section. Space owners can create challenges specific to their space, separate from platform-wide challenges. Space content appears in members' feeds. The session noted "a great number of spaces present" and described space challenges appearing in a stories-style horizontal scroll format. Joining a space is a single tap with immediate content integration.
**Why it does not map to an existing mechanic:** Spaces share structural elements with `community-groups` (named membership, shared content feed, join/leave mechanics) but add creator-driven challenge infrastructure (space owners can create and administer their own challenges with their own rules), tool bookmarking, and a dual-scale challenge system (platform + space). The space challenge creation capability specifically — where any space owner can run a community competition — is not described in the `community-groups` or `challenges` library entries.
**Worth adding to library:** Map to `community-groups` with a note flagging the user-generated challenge capability within spaces as a notable extension of the mechanic. The creator-run challenge infrastructure within community spaces is worth calling out as a distinct variant.
