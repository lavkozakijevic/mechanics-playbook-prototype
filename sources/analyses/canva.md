## Overview

Canva is a browser and mobile design tool for creating visual content — social media posts, presentations, websites, logos, and more. It operates on a freemium model with a perpetually available free tier and two paid tiers (Canva Pro, Canva Business). The iOS app provides the full design environment, a template library, an AI generation suite, an integrated app marketplace, and a learning platform called Design School with certifications. The session covered onboarding through extended feature exploration across most major sections of the app.

---

## First impression

The first prompt is Apple's tracking permission dialog. Sign-in is offered via Apple, Google, Facebook, email, Microsoft, work email, or phone number. After selecting Apple sign-in, a four-step onboarding flow runs: (1) use case selection — Personal, Work/Business, Education, or Nonprofit; (2) notification opt-in with a preview of what notifications look like; (3) an AI consent screen with a pre-toggled-on setting for "improve our AI tools for everyone with your content"; (4) on accepting, confetti falls and the home screen loads. The home screen lands on the Templates tab. No product tour or guided walkthrough follows — the user is left to explore independently.

---

## Core activity

Canva's primary activity is creating and editing visual designs from templates or from scratch. On mobile, the editing experience covers template customization, element addition, text editing, AI image and website generation, sharing, and publishing. A secondary activity — Design School — provides courses, lessons, guides, and certifications for learning Canva and design skills. The two activities are loosely connected: Design School course activities open live Canva templates for hands-on practice.

## Mechanics observed

### Achievements (`achievements`) · Supporting

**What was observed:**
Design School contains a dedicated Achievements section with named, permanent markers for course and certificate completion.

**Badges observed with named tiers:**
- Learning milestones: First Course / Three Courses / Five Courses
- Certificate milestones: First Certificate / Three Certificates / Five Certificates
- Community sharing: Sharing Superstar / LinkedIn Official

Badges are grayed out until earned. Each unearned badge has a single call-to-action button: "Browse Courses."

The Achievements section also shows aggregate stats: skills completed (count), lessons completed (count), courses completed (count), certificates earned (count and issue date).

During the session, the user completed an AI Skills for Students certification quiz (14 questions, 30 minutes allocated). On passing, two sequential popups appeared: "You've hit a learning milestone — you unlocked a badge for earning your first certificate. Proof that you're building something great." followed by "You're Canva Certified — here's your AI Skills for Students certificate of completion. We'll email you a copy too." Confetti appeared on completion.

The certificate itself states: "Certificate of Completion — Canva Certified. The certificate holder has completed the AI Skills for Students course, equivalent to 1 hour 30 minutes of training." It includes the date and a credential ID. The certificate can be shared on social media or downloaded as an image.

The LinkedIn Official badge specifically names a platform as its sharing target — not just "share on social" but LinkedIn by name. The Sharing Superstar badge incentivizes social sharing more broadly.

**How it is presented:**
Achievements are inside Design School, accessed via the sidebar. The section has four sub-tabs: Overview (aggregate stats), Badges, Certificates, and Skills. Badges are displayed as a grid of grayed-out icons until earned. No in-design achievements were described — this system is entirely within Design School.

**What is notable:**
The LinkedIn Official badge names a specific external platform as the sharing target — this is the most specific social sharing incentive observed across any app in this library. The certification is externally shareable with a credential ID, giving it verifiable legitimacy beyond a purely in-app marker. The quiz was passable with "common sense" according to the transcript, suggesting the barrier to earning the First Certificate badge is intentionally low.

### Challenges (`challenges`) · Shallow

**What was observed:**
Design School course activities function as opt-in structured objectives with defined completion states and rewards (badges, certificates). The certification quiz observed — AI Skills for Students — has 14 questions, a 30-minute window, a pass/fail outcome, and a named reward (the First Certificate badge and a downloadable certificate). Entry is voluntary; the user chose to take the quiz without completing the full course first.

Individual Design School courses are described with duration, difficulty level, lesson count, and whether they carry a shareable certificate. Some are free, some are premium (marked with a crown). Content types include courses, lessons, guides, activities, cheat sheets, and videos.

**How it is presented:**
Accessed through Design School sidebar navigation. Courses are filterable by audience, skill, duration, content type, plan type, device, and Canva role. The quiz was launched from within a course detail page.

**What is notable:**
The overlap between this mechanic and `achievements` is significant — course completion produces both the achievement badge and the certificate. The challenges mechanic is logged separately because each quiz/course has the defining features of challenges: opt-in, visible endpoint, reward. However, the evidence is thin — only one quiz was described in detail.

### Variable Reward (`variable-reward`) · Shallow

**What was observed:**
The AI Magic Media tool generates four outputs simultaneously in response to a text prompt. The session described generating a 3D element: "there are four results in 3D." The user does not know in advance which specific outputs will be generated — the prompt produces a set of results that vary each time. This fits the variable reward definition only loosely: the user sets the prompt and receives unknown outputs, which is uncertainty by design. However, the framing is utility (generate what I need) rather than reward (open to see what you get).

The Discovery Queue equivalent in Canva is the template browser with infinite scroll and the "More templates for you" section at the bottom of the home feed. Templates are surfaced editorially and the user does not know in advance what will appear next.

These are weak instances of the variable reward mechanic as defined in the library. Logged at Shallow to note their presence without overstating the evidence.

## Mechanics not observed

**Daily Login Reward:** No reward for opening the app each day was described. No calendar, no daily claim mechanic.

**Streak:** No consecutive-day counter or streak with a reset mechanic was described.

**Season Pass:** No dual-tier reward track advanced through in-app actions was described. The free trial (14 days for Pro or Business) is a time-limited access offer, not a progression track.

**Energy-Lives:** No cap on creative actions per time window was described. The AI generation tools have usage limits (the paywall copy references "10 times more AI usage than Canva Free" for Pro and "20x more AI usage" for Business), but these are subscription-tier caps, not a regenerating attempt mechanic.

**Daily-Weekly Quests:** No recurring structured objectives with a reset cadence were described.

**Leaderboards:** No ranked list with a named metric and visible user position was described.

**Clans-Guilds:** Teams are described as a feature (people management, team collaboration) but no shared goals, collective reward tracks, or contribution mechanics were described.

**Piggy Bank / Ads / Gifting:** None observed.

## How mechanics connect

**Design School achievements drive external sharing.** The LinkedIn Official and Sharing Superstar badges specifically reward sharing certificates and completed courses to external platforms. The certificate's social share functionality and downloadable image format are purpose-built for this — the achievement system is partly an acquisition funnel, rewarding users for promoting Canva's educational platform to their networks.

**Challenges produce achievements.** Every Design School course or quiz that carries a certificate produces both the certificate (an external credential) and the First Certificate badge (an in-app achievement). The two mechanics are inseparable at the point of completion — finishing one always produces both.

Canva is a creation tool — session rhythm is driven by project work rather than engagement design. No timed return incentives were observed. These are the stated return triggers — project collaboration events and editorial template recommendations, not daily reward mechanics.

## What stood out

1. **The per-element credit system sits alongside the subscription as a parallel monetization path.** For a user who needs one premium image for one design, 1 credit at $1.99 is positioned as the low-friction alternative to a $18/month subscription. The credit drop-down reveals 8 pricing tiers from $1.99 to $99.99, with increasingly favorable per-credit rates at higher volumes. This is a deliberate pay-per-use path for non-subscribers that coexists with the subscription rather than replacing it.

2. **The paywall copy adapts to the user's entry point.** When the user hit the paywall through the brand kit feature, it said "upgrade to get up to 100 brand kits." When through an AI tool, it described AI usage limits. The paywall is context-sensitive — it always frames the upgrade in terms of what the user was just trying to do, not as a general feature list.

3. **The AI consent toggle is pre-enabled at onboarding.** The screen during onboarding states "with this setting on, we may learn from your content to build even better AI tools for everyone" and presents a toggle that is already in the on position. The user must actively opt out rather than actively opt in. This is described in the transcript without comment — the confetti falls on accepting, which immediately follows this screen.

4. **The LinkedIn Official badge is the most platform-specific social sharing incentive observed across the library.** Rather than a generic "share on social" reward, Canva has named LinkedIn specifically as the target for one of its sharing badges — directly incentivizing sharing credentials on the professional network where Canva certifications are most likely to carry signal. The Sharing Superstar badge handles other platforms.

## Unrecognized mechanics

### `freemium-content-gating`
**What was observed:** Throughout the session, premium content and features are marked with a crown icon wherever they appear — in the template browser, the photo library, the brand kit, sharing options, and analytics. Crown icons are described as appearing consistently: "there's no confusion that we don't know that this is about to be a premium feature for us — so that there's no confusion." Tapping any crown-marked item opens a contextual paywall that describes the upgrade in terms of the feature the user was attempting to access. Three resolution paths exist: subscribe to Pro ($18/month), subscribe to Business ($25/month), purchase a per-element credit ($1.99–$99.99), or for some content, download with a Canva watermark for free. The watermarked download is a free fallback that delivers the functionality at reduced quality.
**Why it does not map to an existing mechanic:** Not `energy-lives` (no regenerating cap). Not `season-pass` (no progression track). Not `credits-tokens` (credits are purchased and spent directly on specific elements, not as an abstract intermediary layer — though they are structurally similar). This is a freemium content-access gating system where premium content is permanently visible but watermarked or paywalled, with multiple payment paths to resolution. The crown icon system and contextual paywall copy are design patterns worth naming as a mechanic distinct from other paywall approaches.
**Worth adding to library:** Yes — freemium content gating with persistent in-catalogue visibility (always visible, never hidden) and contextual paywall copy is a distinct pattern from hard paywalls, energy gates, or subscription-only access.

### `credits-tokens` (Canva Credits)
**What was observed:** Canva Credits are purchased with real money (1 credit for $1.99, scaling to 100 credits for $99.99) and spent on individual premium elements within designs (1 credit per premium image or element). Credits are described as "only needed for one design — pay for it once." The credit system exists as an explicit alternative to the subscription: "don't pay for images — get Canva Business" appears next to the credit purchase option, positioning them as competing paths. A per-element credit cost of 1 credit was stated.
**Why it does not map to an existing mechanic:** This actually fits the `credits-tokens` definition closely — real money is first converted to credits, which are then spent separately on items. The conversion step (dollars → credits → elements) is the defining feature. Logged here as an unrecognized mechanic note rather than a full mechanic entry because the session only briefly described the credit system, and the primary observation is that it coexists with a subscription rather than replacing it — a structural pattern worth noting for the library entry on `credits-tokens`.
**Worth adding to library:** Map to existing `credits-tokens` mechanic. Flag the coexistence-with-subscription pattern as a notable implementation variant.

### `in-app-education-with-certification`
**What was observed:** Design School is an integrated learning platform within Canva offering courses, lessons, guides, activities, cheat sheets, and videos across dozens of skill areas (animation, branding, content creation, data visualization, graphic design, UX, etc.). Courses carry verifiable completion certificates with credential IDs, issuable as shareable images. The achievement system (badges, stats) tracks completion across the platform. Certification quizzes are passable through "common sense" according to the transcript. The stated purpose is dual: learning the tool and earning externally shareable proof of that learning. Course activities open live Canva templates for practice, directly connecting the learning content to the product's core creation activity.
**Why it does not map to an existing mechanic:** Not `achievements` — Design School is the platform that generates the achievements, not an achievement system itself. Not `challenges` — while individual quizzes fit the challenges definition, Design School as a whole is an educational infrastructure with multiple content types, filterable catalogues, and a credential system. It is a product-integrated educational platform that uses achievement and challenge mechanics as its reward layer, not a mechanic itself. Worth noting because embedding a full certification platform inside a design tool (rather than linking to external training) is an unusual and significant engagement pattern.
**Worth adding to library:** Possibly — as a category-level observation for productivity and creative tools that embed educational platforms to deepen user investment in learning the tool.
