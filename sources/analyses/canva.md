# Canva behavioral design analysis

**Session date:** 25 Apr 2026
**Additional sessions:** None.
**As observed:** 25 Apr 2026
**App version:** None.
**Analysis date:** 13 Sep 2026
**Last updated:** 13 Sep 2026

Source: one narrated walkthrough of the Canva mobile app, recorded on 25 Apr 2026 and held in Granola as "App Mechanics: Canva". The session runs continuously from first launch and account creation through template and photo browsing, the editor, the AI generation tools, export, sharing and commenting, website publishing, the apps directory, Design School and its certification flow, account settings, and the Canva Business paywall. It is a single session on one account with no prior Canva state on the device. Every statement in this analysis is drawn from that transcript and from nothing else.

## Onboarding and first run

**O1. Canva asks for cross-app tracking consent before anything else.**

**Observed:** The first prompt on opening the app asks the user to allow Canva to track their activity across other companies' apps and websites. It appears before any product screen (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- It is the first pop-up of the session, ahead of the sign-up screen (tier: directly observed).
- The session does not record which option was chosen or whether the choice changed anything later (tier: unresolved).

**O2. The product requires an account before it can be used.**

**Observed:** The first Canva screen is "Continue to sign up for free. If you already have an account, we'll log you in." No path into the product without an account is shown (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Visible options: Apple, Google, Facebook, email, and "continue another way" (tier: directly observed).
- "Continue another way" opens Microsoft, Clever, work email login, and login with phone number (tier: directly observed).
- The screen collapses sign-up and login into one action, so the user does not have to know which they are doing (tier: directly observed).
- The session proceeded with Apple (tier: directly observed).

**O3. Canva asks what the product will be used for and attaches free-premium eligibility to two of the answers.**

**Observed:** After sign-in, the app says "So let's create a Canva design for you. What will you be using Canva for?" and offers four answers, two of which state that premium features are free for that group (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Personal projects: "tools and templates to create what you love" (tier: directly observed).
- Work and business: "where productivity meets creativity" (tier: directly observed).
- Education: "students and teachers can get premium features for free" (tier: directly observed).
- Nonprofit: "800,000 nonprofits get premium features for free" (tier: directly observed).
- The session selected personal projects, so no eligibility check for the two free-premium categories was reached (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O31.*

**O4. AI training consent is presented as an explanation with the toggle already on.**

**Observed:** A screen headed "Before you start, let's talk AI" tells the user that AI is integrated throughout Canva, that it is improved with the help of people like them, and that "with this setting on, we may learn from your content to build even better AI tools for everyone." Beneath it sits a toggle reading "improve our AI tools for everyone with your content", already switched on (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The default state is on; opting out requires the user to act (tier: directly observed).
- The copy offers a later exit: "You can opt out now or anytime in settings where you're always in control of your privacy and AI preferences" (tier: directly observed).
- The framing is collective rather than personal: the benefit named is better AI tools "for everyone" (tier: directly observed).
- Accepting produced confetti across the screen (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O18.*

**O5. Onboarding ends by dropping the user into a full template catalogue.**

**Observed:** Immediately after the AI consent screen the user lands on the home surface, which opens on Templates. No tutorial, sample project or guided first design follows; the first unguided screen is a browsable catalogue (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The user's profile sits in the top left and a crown in the top right (tier: directly observed).
- The speaker read the crown as marking premium content or features (tier: plausible, the session did not open it at this point).
- Guidance stops here: from this screen on, nothing in the session tells the user what to do next (tier: directly observed).

## Core loop and automation

**O6. The home surface is an endless template and content catalogue.**

**Observed:** Home opens on Templates and scrolls without a visible end, mixing category shortcuts, promotional rows and further template grids (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Top-level tabs: templates, photos, graphics, creators, store content (tier: directly observed).
- Tapping the arrow next to Templates reveals a search bar beneath it (tier: directly observed).
- Category options include business, social media, video, explore templates, TikTok video, logo, Instagram story, Instagram post, poster, invitation, email and flyer, among others (tier: directly observed).
- Scrolling passes "Meet AI video effects", "Discover Canva", "Canva's top picks" with items such as standout business cards, statement stickers, short videos and authentic stories, a row offering Disney content, and then "More templates for you" (tier: directly observed).
- The speaker described the scroll as continuing infinitely (tier: strongly supported, the session scrolled at length without reaching an end).
- The category bar reappears above the content as the user scrolls down (tier: directly observed).

**O7. The photo library is browsable by collection and marks premium items in place.**

**Observed:** The photos tab presents curated collections and a stated library size, with crowns on individual premium items. Opening an item shows a detail sheet with licensing terms and onward actions (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Collections observed: Day of life, Entrepreneur, Year in review, Poppy work from home (tier: directly observed).
- The surface states more than 240,000 photos are available (tier: directly observed).
- A crown next to an image marks it as premium (tier: directly observed).
- The detail sheet carries a description, the photo's size, who provided it, and a Pro content licence reading "don't resell or take credit for the original media or trademark anything that contains it. Use this media in a Canva design for a wide range of personal commercial uses" (tier: directly observed).
- Available actions: star, report, add to folder, use in design, plus a row of similar images (tier: directly observed).
- The premium preview shows the image with a Canva mark over it, which the speaker corrected from "watermark" to a preview of how it looks (tier: directly observed).

## Goals and progression

**O19. Design School is a separate section of the app with its own navigation.**

**Observed:** Design School opens what the speaker described as a new section of the app, with its own sidebar and its own catalogue, headed "Design school is in session. Learn how to design in Canva with free resources and certified courses" (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Sidebar structure: explore all, courses, lessons, achievements, plus new in Canva, Learn blog and events (tier: directly observed).
- Landing prompts: "What do you want to learn", new in Canva, get started, certified courses, video editing, template customization, and a "picked for you" row (tier: directly observed).
- Content types: activity, cheat sheet, course, guide, lesson, video (tier: directly observed).
- Filters: audience, skill, duration, certified courses, getting started, plan, device, Canva role, visual suite, features (tier: directly observed).
- Skills covered include animation, brainstorming, branding, content creation, creating activities, data visualization, design thinking, graphic design, infographics, layout, photo editing, typography and user experience (tier: directly observed).
- Audiences listed: content creators, design professionals, knowledge workers, marketers, sales teams, small business owners, solo and personal, students, teachers (tier: directly observed).
- Lessons run roughly ten to fifteen minutes; durations filter from under five minutes to more than an hour (tier: directly observed).
- The speaker judged the material substantial rather than promotional, with tips, additional resources, suggested further guides and step-by-step instructions (tier: plausible, this is the analyst's assessment of the content rather than a product behavior).

**O20. Every course states its terms before the user starts.**

**Observed:** Each course card shows whether it is premium or free, how long it takes, its difficulty level, whether it carries a shareable certificate, and how many lessons it contains (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Course pages also carry what you learn, key skills, a description, when it was last updated, a share action, and an FAQ at the bottom (tier: directly observed).
- A "stand out with this course" prompt sits on the page (tier: directly observed).
- The shareable certificate is disclosed as a property of the course before any work is done (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O21, O22.*

**O21. The certification test is a bounded, stated instance that can be taken without the course.**

**Observed:** "Earn a certificate" opens a certification test that states its terms and can be started directly: "Test your knowledge of the AI skills for students course with multiple choice questions and on successful completion be awarded a certificate. Let's start." The session started it without having taken the course (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Stated terms: 14 questions, lasts 30 minutes, carries a certificate of completion (tier: directly observed).
- The course itself was not completed first, and nothing blocked entry to the test (tier: directly observed).
- Progress is visible throughout: the user can see which question they are on (tier: directly observed).
- Each correct selection returns "Great" and an explanation of why that answer is correct (tier: directly observed).
- The speaker found the questions easy to answer by common sense (tier: plausible, one analyst's judgement on one test).
- What happens on failure, whether retakes are allowed, and what the pass threshold is were not observed (tier: unresolved).

**O22. Completion produces a badge and a dated credential in two steps.**

**Observed:** Finishing the test produced confetti, then a pop-up reading "You've hit a learning milestone. You unlocked a badge for earning your first certificate. Proof that you're building something great", then a second screen reading "You're Canva certified. Here's your AI skills for students certificate of completion. We'll email you a copy too" (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The badge and the certificate are separate awards presented in sequence, badge first (tier: directly observed).
- The certificate reads "certificate of completion, Canva certified" and states that "the certificate holder has completed the AI skills for students course, which is the equivalent to 1 hour 30 minutes of training" (tier: directly observed).
- It carries a date, a credential ID, and the line "presented by the Canva team, Canva Design School" (tier: directly observed).
- The certificate can be shared to social or downloaded as an image (tier: directly observed).
- A copy is sent to the user's email as well as shown in the app (tier: directly observed, stated by the product; delivery not verified in the session).
- The stated training equivalent, 1 hour 30 minutes, was not the time spent: the credential was earned from the test alone (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O24, O40.*

## Access and eligibility

**O27. A crown marks premium content everywhere it appears.**

**Observed:** Crowns appear on the home surface, on individual photos, on editor tools, on custom link creation and on connected advanced tools, marking each as premium before it is opened (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The speaker described the marking as consistent enough that there is no confusion about what will be gated (tier: strongly supported, crowns were observed across five separate surfaces in the session).
- The marker sits on the item, not only at the point of payment, so the boundary is visible while browsing (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O5, O7, O32, O34.*

**O28. The brand kit is fully browsable and entirely locked.**

**Observed:** Brand opens into a complete set of categories, each with explanatory copy, and each ending in an "Upgrade to Canva Business" button rather than a working control (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Categories: brand templates, guidelines, assets, logos, colours, fonts, brand voice, photos, components, graphics, icons, charts (tier: directly observed).
- Guidelines reads "add brand guidelines to stay consistent. Include the brand story, values and standards on how to use each asset so your brand shines" (tier: directly observed).
- Photos reads "stay on brand your way. Add brand assets or guidelines for consistency across designs. Rename this category as you wish" (tier: directly observed).
- Fonts reads "add fonts to express your brand. Define how each font should appear in designs to have a clear and consistent look" (tier: directly observed).
- From the editor, brand offers "try business for 14 days" or "start with three free colours"; choosing three free colours leads to a palette that itself requires the Business upgrade (tier: directly observed).
- The free path therefore ends at the same gate as the paid path (tier: strongly supported).

*Cross-reference: O9, O43.*

**O29. Free export of premium content is possible, with the watermark left on.**

**Detail:**

- Tapping the premium image in the canvas surfaces a "remove watermarks" control (tier: directly observed).
- The gate is at output, not at use: the user can build the whole design with premium content before meeting it (tier: strongly supported).
- Downloading asks for permission to access the user's photos (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**

## Social

**O34. A design's sharing surface is an access list controlled by its owner.**

**Observed:** Share settings let the user add names, groups or emails, show who owns the design and who currently has access, offer "anyone with the link", and offer "remove access for everyone", described as removing all users with access and making the design private so that only the owner retains it (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Access is granted per design and per person; the session showed no state shared across designs (tier: directly observed).
- Copy link is free; creating a custom link is crowned, and claiming one opens the "try Canva Business for free" plan chooser (tier: directly observed).
- The custom link is built as a Canva link, a slash, and text the user supplies (tier: directly observed).
- Other routes from the same surface: invite people to collaborate, share a public view link, present, copy to clipboard, download the selection (tier: directly observed).
- This is a permission list attached to one artifact rather than a group the user belongs to: revoking access ends access to that design and to nothing else (tier: strongly supported).

*Cross-reference: O36, O39.*

**O35. Comments attach to a chosen point in the design and carry their own lifecycle.**

**Observed:** The user can add a comment, choose where in the design it sits, write it with text styles, tag people, add emojis and comment stickers, and post it. Posted comments can be resolved, edited, deleted, reported, and have a link copied to them (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The comment is anchored to a position the user selects, not to the design as a whole (tier: directly observed).
- Comments persist on the design and can be tapped to view later (tier: directly observed).
- Tagging brings a named person into a specific comment (tier: directly observed).
- Resolution is a state on the comment, distinct from deletion (tier: directly observed).
- A per-comment link exists, so a single comment can be pointed at from outside (tier: directly observed).
- No second participant took part in the session, so the mechanic was observed as controls rather than as an exchange (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O34.*

**O36. Canva maintains teams with managed membership.**

**Observed:** The account area shows the teams the user belongs to, and a people management screen showing who has been invited to work in the team (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The product speaks in the plural, "teams that we have", so a user can hold membership in more than one (tier: directly observed).
- Membership is created by invitation, and people management is where it is administered (tier: strongly supported).
- The session did not show what team membership changes: no member-scoped content, permission or shared state was opened (tier: directly observed).
- Adjacent account items on the same screen: orders, invoices, subscription, web domains (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O34.*

## Reach beyond the app

**O37. Finished work exports into other companies' products directly.**

**Observed:** The share and download surface sends a design to Facebook, Microsoft PowerPoint, OneDrive, Google Drive and Dropbox, as well as to a website, social channels and messaging (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Destinations named: Facebook profile, Microsoft PowerPoint, OneDrive, Google Drive, Dropbox, add to website, share on social, send via messaging (tier: directly observed).
- A screenshot of the result offered "save to camera roll" and "share this design" in the same pop-up (tier: directly observed).
- That pop-up also displayed a Mark Twain quote, "good friends, good books and a sleepy conscience, this is the ideal life", which the speaker could not account for (tier: directly observed).

**O38. A design can be published as a live website from inside the app.**

**Observed:** Publishing a design as a website offers a my-Canva-site domain, a claimable free Canva domain, or an external domain, and exposes site-level settings before publishing (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Settings: title and favicon, a description under 160 words used as the meta description, search engine visibility, password protection, social media link preview, each a toggle (tier: directly observed).
- Claiming a free Canva domain requires typing the domain and claiming it (tier: directly observed).
- Publishing re-raises the premium element payment, with the counter-offer "don't pay for images. Get Canva Business. Unlock this image and 90 million more. Start your free trial now" (tier: directly observed).

# Close: system view

Canva's system has two halves that meet at one point, and the point is export.

The first half is the production loop and it is almost entirely unobstructed. The catalogue at O6 and O7 opens straight after onboarding with no tutorial, the format picker at O8 turns any browsed asset into a started design, and the editor at O9 lets the user build with premium content freely. The generative surfaces extend the same permissiveness: Magic Media at O10 will write the prompt for the user, Canva AI at O11 will build an entire website from one sentence. Nothing in this half rations activity. There is no capacity limit, no cooldown, no daily allowance, and no schedule the user has to meet. That absence is the single most consistent finding in the record, and it is why twenty of the library's entries were never observed.

The second half is the gate, and every route through it converges at output. The crown at O27 keeps the boundary visible during production, so the user knows what will cost while they build, but nothing stops them building with it. The charge arrives at export (O29), at watermark removal (O45) and again at publishing (O38, O48), always after the work exists. Three priced routes cross the same gate: the 14-day trial at O44, the one-off licence at O46, and the credit balance at O33 and O47. They are not tiers of one offer; they are alternatives that the product itself arbitrates between, to the point of arguing against its own credit path on the screen where the credit price is displayed (O48). The paywall's headline is written from wherever the user entered it (O43), which means the gate is described to each user in terms of the thing they were denied rather than in terms of what is sold.

Design School is a third structure and it sits apart from both. It is the only place in the product where anything accumulates: the counters at O23, the tiered badges at O24, the credential at O22. Everything the library recognises as progression in Canva lives here, and none of it touches the production loop. Completing courses does not change what the editor can do, does not affect the gate, and does not alter the credit price. What it does instead is point outward: the credential is built to leave (O40), and two of the badges are earned only by sending Canva somewhere else. The dependency runs one way: the badge tiers at O24 depend on the counters at O23, the counters depend on completing courses, and the certificate at O22 depends on a test that can be taken without completing any (O21). That last dependency is broken in the product's favour, which is worth stating plainly because it is what makes the credential cheap to obtain and therefore easy to share.

Three friction points recur and none of them sit in the production loop. The record of what the user has earned is hard to get back to (O25). The home surface changed between the first and second launch without explanation (O16). A control overlay trapped the user and forced a restart at the payment step (O17). All three fall on the surfaces the user reaches after the work is done, which is the same region of the product where the charge sits.

The social surfaces are thin and artifact-scoped. Sharing, access and comments (O34, O35) all attach to one design; teams (O36) exist but the session never reached anything membership changes; analytics (O39) count an audience the user has to create by distributing the design elsewhere. Canva's social layer is built around moving a file outward, not around holding people together inside the product, which is consistent with the certificate, the sharing badges, the apps directory and the export destinations all pointing the same way.
