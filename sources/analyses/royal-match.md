## Onboarding and first run

**O1. Tracking permission is the first thing the app asks for.**

**Observed:** Royal Match opens in vertical orientation and, before anything else, asks whether the user allows Royal Match to track their activity across other companies' apps and websites (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The tracking request precedes any gameplay, any account step and any explanation of the game (tier: directly observed).
- No notification permission is requested at this point (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O2, O62.*

## Core loop and automation

**O10. The level as the repeating unit: a target and a move limit, with no clock.**

**Observed:** Each level states what must be achieved and how many moves remain, and carries no timer (tier: directly observed). Reaching the target within the moves completes the level; running out of moves ends it as a failure (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The narration states explicitly that there is no timer, only a target and remaining moves (tier: directly observed).
- Completing a level grants one star (tier: directly observed).
- A failed level can be started again (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O13, O14, O40.*

**O11. The board objective changes from level to level.**

**Observed:** Royal Match changes what must be destroyed or collected as levels progress (tier: directly observed). Observed objectives include breaking a box, collecting 36 wooden pieces, destroying patches of grass, making matches next to mailboxes to collect mail, and breaking royal eggs by matching next to them (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Mailboxes are not destroyed; each qualifying match collects mail from adjacent mailboxes, which rewards playing next to them (tier: directly observed).
- The hard level at 39 is composed of three mailboxes (tier: directly observed).
- The narration treats the rotation of objectives as the way the game stays varied (tier: plausible, the narrator's reading).

*Cross-reference: O19.*

**O12. Difficulty escalates, and failure arrives around level 18 to 21.**

**Observed:** Royal Match increases difficulty gradually, and the narrator reaches level 10 without using any booster (tier: directly observed). The first failures occur from level 18 onward: a level is failed once, level 19 is failed twice in a row, and the narration describes the game as significantly harder after level 21 (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Before the first failure the narrator banks boosters rather than spending them (tier: directly observed).
- The narration describes saving resources as satisfying for an experienced player and as a safety net for a new one (tier: plausible, the narrator's reading of the experience, not a product behavior).
- The narration reads the placement of the first hard failures as the point where a player who has been winning feels the sting (tier: plausible, an interpretation with no user evidence behind it).

*Cross-reference: O13, O14, O21, O33.*

## Applied tags

**Tag:** Lives

**Observations:** O13, O41.

**Supporting observations:** O10, O12, O14, O31, O50, O57, O63.

**Confidence:** directly observed.

**Role:** engagement, retention, monetization.

Hearts decide whether the next attempt can begin at all (O13), the regeneration timer sets when the user can come back (O63), and the refill price and the unlimited-hearts periods in the offers are what the app sells against the shortage (O14, O57).

**Rationale:** Royal Match holds five discrete hearts, removes one when a level is failed, and the remaining count governs whether another attempt can start (tier: directly observed). Restoration is defined at 24 to 25 minutes per heart, with a 900-coin refill and teammate requests as alternatives (tier: directly observed). Consumption is tied to failure rather than to entry: levels are started freely and hearts are reported lost at the failures from level 18 onward (tier: strongly supported, no heart is observed being consumed on entry).

**Variants present:** failure-loss lives, regenerating lives, purchasable lives, earned lives (teammate transfers), shared lives in the sense of teammate supply, unlimited-lives state (the infinite-hearts periods sold in offers and granted by the pass), and a raised bound of eight under the activated Easter Pass.

**Variants not established:** protected lives; mode-specific lives; variable-loss lives; scheduled-reset lives. The state at zero hearts was never reached, so what the lower boundary does is not observed.

**Alternative considered:** Energy, which would apply if a heart were consumed on starting a level rather than on failing it. The session separates them: levels are entered and re-entered without a heart being taken until a level is failed.

**Tag:** Progression Gate

**Observations:** O34, O35, O36.

**Role:** retention, engagement.

**Variants present:** level gate, browsable gate (the teams surface opens and shows its contents while locked), opaque gate (the locked items cannot be tapped), undescribed gate (the collection lock explains nothing about what it withholds), staggered gates (the items release across levels 8 to 18), announced unlock gate (each item unlock is followed by an instruction at the level where it first becomes usable).

**Variants not established:** count gate; stage gate; composite gate; previewed gate; reinstating gate; per-position gate; shared-measure gate; bypassable gate. No purchase or alternative route past any of these gates is observed.

**Alternative considered:** Milestone, which covers the product's recognition that a defined point in a progression has been reached. It is not applied to these three, because a gate is the standing condition on a withheld part rather than the recognition event at the crossing. The team tournament's requirement of at least ten members was also considered and is excluded: roster size is not a measure the user advances by playing, so it is an eligibility condition and is recorded in plain language at O37.

## Entries considered and not applied

- Energy. Hearts are the candidate and they are consumed when a level is failed rather than when one is started, which the entry directs to Lives; no separate action-consumption capacity is observed (O13).
- Group Membership. The team maintains membership and governance, but it also holds collective state of its own, and the decision ladder for the three sibling social entries gives the single label to Clan / Guild (O48).
- Community Space. The team message board is a persistent shared context, but the same ladder applies and the more specific sibling feature is present, so this label does not apply to that surface (O49).
- Social Feed. The team message board was weighed against this entry. What the session shows is a group conversation and a set of standing help requests inside one bounded team context, not a selected and ordered stream of items from multiple actors; the entry excludes a bounded conversation (O49).
- Spendable Credits and Tokens. The parent entry of the two currency labels applied. Its invariant is satisfied by gold coins and gold stars, and under the overlap rule the more specific labels, Hard Currency and Soft Currency, are reported instead, with the parent noted in each (O39, O40).
- Progression Fund. Considered for the Easter Pass. It is excluded because the track carries an event boundary that ends it, and because it runs on keys of its own rather than on a measure the product already maintains for another purpose (O31).
- Season Content Pass. Considered for the Easter Pass. It is excluded because holding the entitlement pays larger rewards at track positions rather than granting access to a grouped body of content items (O31, O60).
- Daily Claim Pack. Considered for the Easter Treasures ladder. It is excluded because the rungs are released by claiming the previous rung, not by recurring daily or weekly intervals within an entitlement period, and because no purchase establishes the ladder in the first place (O59).
- Daily / Weekly Quests. Considered for the area tasks and the event steps. No daily or weekly issuance, refresh or reset boundary is observed anywhere in the session; the area tasks appear once each and the event steps advance on play (O23, O29).
- Daily Login Rewards. No benefit is observed being granted for accessing the product during a daily window, including on the one occasion the narrator reopens the app after a break (O62).
- Passive Construction. Considered for the castle and area building. It is excluded because spending the stars completes the build immediately, with an animation and no intervening process that advances on its own (O23).
- Achievement. Considered for the profile stats, the area completions and the chef badge. The profile keeps running counts rather than attained states, and the area unlock follows automatically from progression; the chef badge is the one candidate for a preserved attained state and is recorded as unresolved (O28, O24, O32).
- Minigame. Considered for the King's Nightmare interludes, which run on rules of their own, sit between levels and can be skipped. It is excluded because each one pays 50 coins into the ordinary coin balance that the core loop spends from, and an activity that feeds the core economy is loop activity whatever its rules or its placement. The bounded objective inside each interlude is carried by Challenge instead (O17, O39).
- Rewarded Advertisement. No advertisement appears anywhere in the session. The King's Nightmare interludes resemble advertising creative for the game, which is the reverse relationship, and the coins they pay are conditional on playing a mini-activity rather than on engaging with an advertisement (O17).
- Variable Reward Schedule. Considered for the arrival of the interludes, the unlocks and the events. Every observed arrival is tied to a level number, which is consistent with a fixed rule the session simply did not see stated; the entry directs that irregular-looking timing is not enough to separate variable scheduling from a hidden fixed rule (O15, O17, O22, O29, O31, O32).
- Personal Data Reflection. Considered for the profile's general stats. The stats are displayed for viewing; no step requires a response from the user, which the entry requires (O28).
- Paywalled Reward Ladder. Not a library entry. It was proposed from this analysis on 13 Sep 2026 and rejected on 14 Sep 2026, and it is recorded here so it is not proposed again. The Easter Treasures surface reveals each rung by claiming the previous one, most rungs cost nothing, and one rung must be purchased before the free rungs beyond it can be reached (O59). The structure exists to place a purchase in the user's path, and a commercial or billing arrangement is not a tag however it is presented, so the ladder is described in plain language in the observation record and carries no label. A future analyst meeting this shape in another app should record the claim sequence, the paid rung and the window in Pass one and stop there.

Royal Match runs one activity and pays it into many places. Every measure in the game is advanced by the same act of clearing a match-three level: the player level (O26), the star that buys the next piece of castle (O40), the coins that pay to undo failure (O39), and every event unit the app is running at the time (O25, O42). Nothing else the user can do produces progress. This is what makes the level the load-bearing observation of the whole record: the tags on it, Challenge and Lives, are attached to the only faucet in the system.

The near loop is short and self-contained. A level states a target and a move limit (O10), pays one star (O40), and the star pays for one step of the current area (O23). The area holds six to nine steps and ends in a chest and a new area (O24), inside a map of 152 (O27). The cost of a step doubles early, from one star to two (O8, O23), so the distance between visible changes to the castle widens while the level itself stays the same length. The building prompt can be dismissed and stars can bank instead (O23), which means the castle is a sink the user chooses to feed rather than a gate on play, and the marker on the area chest exists to keep an unfed sink visible (O66).

Failure is where the economy turns. Hearts are consumed only when a level is failed (O13), and the app's own pacing delays the first failure until around level 18 (O12), by which point the level count has already released the assist items (O36), the rating prompt has already been shown after a clean run (O65), and the team offer has already been described in terms of free lives (O34). When failure does arrive it produces two priced exits at the same number, 900 coins for five more moves and 900 coins for a heart refill (O14, O13), against a coin balance that the session shows accumulating slowly: 250 from an area chest, 50 from an interlude, roughly 500 from a whole bonus level (O39, O18). That ratio is what connects the failure observations to the entire monetization section: the coin packs (O56) and the treasure ladder (O57) are priced against a sink the user meets repeatedly, and the offers above the coin packs sell time-limited unlimited hearts and boosters rather than quantities (O57, O58), which converts the failure cost into a rental.

The social layer is bolted to the same failure point. The team's stated value proposition is free lives (O34), the help request is for lives (O49, O50), and the lives panel routes an empty inbox to teammates (O13). Teams are gated at level 21 and the prompt to join arrives at level 20 (O34), one level before it can be acted on and at the point where difficulty has just risen. Once inside, the team supplies what the tournament needs: a roster of at least ten (O37), a collective score the user's lances feed (O33), and a stated rule that non-contributors are paid nothing (O52). The team gift offers then appear immediately after that rule has been shown (O58), which makes the tournament the surface where the social tags, Clan / Guild, Leaderboard, Comparative Rank and Gifting, all sit on the same observations as the monetization role.

The event layer is what converts a finite game into a dated one. Between levels 27 and 41 the app adds five timed structures on top of the loop, one at a time: Propeller Madness (O29), the tournament (O33), Easter Treasures (O59), the Easter Pass (O31) and the Culinary Collection (O32). None of them asks for a new activity; all of them attach a countdown to the activity already being performed (O25, O64). Two of the tags in Pass two exist only because of this layer, Seasonal Progression Pass and Set Collection, and a third, Experience Points, exists entirely inside it. The layer also carries the session's two most consequential gaps: the grant rate per level is never stated for any event measure (O25, O42), and what happens to unclaimed progress when a window closes is never observed (O64). Those two unknowns are what keep the Experience Points tag at strongly supported rather than directly observed.

Two observations sit outside all of this and depend on nothing else in the record. The identity items, avatar, frame and name, are never prompted (O45) and become worth something only once the leaderboards and the team have made them visible to named others (O46, O47), which is also what the activated pass lane sells at its first position (O60). And the King's Nightmare interludes (O17) pay coins into the economy while advancing nothing, which is why they generated a Pass three proposal rather than a tag: the loop they belong to is the advertising loop outside the product, which this session cannot see.
