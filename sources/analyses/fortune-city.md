**App version:** None.
**Analysis date:** 28 Sep 2026

Source. This analysis is built from two Granola walkthrough transcripts of the Fortune City iOS app, both narrated by the analyst while using the app. The first session (13 May 2026, "Fortune City app analysis") covers first launch, onboarding, the guided first steps, the shop, the theme store, the city menus, the finance screens, and roughly ten recorded expenses. The second session (17 May 2026, "Fortune City app review") is a return visit a few days later without visits in between, and covers the return pop-ups, further expense records, the first coin storage upgrade, more merges, and a second pass over the finance screens. The transcripts contain the audio of third-party advertisements played during the sessions; that audio is not a source of facts about Fortune City and is not used. Some product names are rendered inconsistently by the transcription (for example "Sparkful", "Sparkpool" and "Sparkle" for the same account and subscription brand, and "Kashi" and "Cashy" for the mascot); the most frequent rendering is used throughout. Two further Granola notes mention Fortune City in passing (14 May 2026 and 04 Sep 2026); neither is a walkthrough of the app and neither is used. No prior analysis, case study, or general knowledge of the app is used.

## Onboarding and first run

**O1. Welcome screen and entry choice.**

**Observed:** Fortune City opens on a welcome screen stating that in this town every expense can become a building (tier: directly observed). The screen offers two routes, Start and I already have an account (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The product's premise, expenses turning into buildings, is stated on the first screen before any input (tier: directly observed).
- The session took the Start route; the existing-account route was not explored (tier: directly observed).

**O2. First expense as the first action.**

**Observed:** After Start, Fortune City asks for the user's latest expense before any account, permission or profile step (tier: directly observed). The user enters an amount and picks one of ten categories, after which the app announces "Your first building" and states that the transaction has become a drinks building, showing the amount spent on it (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Categories offered: food, drinks, transportation, shopping, entertainment, housing, electronics, medical, miscellaneous, and income (tier: directly observed).
- The session entered a flat white under drinks for 320 dinars (tier: directly observed).
- The resulting screen names the building by category and shows the recorded amount beside it (tier: directly observed).
- A "see demonstration of building" step follows before Continue (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O12, O11.*

**O3. Construction preview.**

**Detail:**

## Core loop and automation

**O11. Expense entry form.**

**Observed:** Recording a new expense opens a form where the user enters an amount, chooses cash or credit card, and picks a category, with preset sub-options under each category (tier: directly observed). A settings panel within the form lets the user reorder the categories (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Food presets include breakfast, brunch, lunch, dinner, afternoon tea, late-night munchies and snacks; drinks presets include coffee, black tea and milk tea; transportation presets include fuel and bus (tier: directly observed).
- Records entered across the sessions included a sticker album (entertainment), fuel for 9,000 dinars by card (transportation), a pen (shopping), and an electronics item (tier: directly observed).
- Income is recorded through the same form as a category (tier: directly observed).

**O12. Records become buildings.**

**Observed:** Each recorded expense adds a building of the matching category to the floating island city, and a new category produces a new kind of store (tier: directly observed). The building appears immediately after the record is saved (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The analyst notes that a new store appears each time a new kind of expense is entered (tier: directly observed).
- Buildings add to prosperity (tier: strongly supported, prosperity threshold achievements fire directly after records are entered).
- The city can be zoomed in and out (tier: directly observed).
- Each category maps to its own kind of building, and the mapping applies to every record converted within the daily builder capacity (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O1, O2, O13, O28.*

**O13. Daily builder capacity.**

**Observed:** Fortune City limits how many records become buildings each day: the first five expenses recorded each day become buildings, and the helmet count in the top bar falls as buildings are made (tier: directly observed). After the fifth, the app states that all builders will be available tomorrow, and further records are still accepted without producing buildings (tier: directly observed). The Builders Hub can be upgraded from five to six buildings per day, and in the second session that upgrade was priced at 500 gems (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The session had 43 gems at first and 87 in the second session, against a 500-gem upgrade price (tier: directly observed).
- The analyst compared the price to the 500-diamond pouch sold for $4.99 and called it not cheap (tier: directly observed).
- The helmet display read as "4.4 out of 5" early in the first session, and later "three out of five" (tier: directly observed).
- Recording itself is never blocked; only the conversion into a building stops (tier: directly observed).
- Whether the next-day refill occurs at a fixed time or on a rolling basis is not observed (tier: unresolved).

*Cross-reference: O10, O30, O55.*

- Category record series: 10 to 100 records of food, drinks, shopping, housing, transportation, travel, electronics; 10 to 50 of medical and miscellaneous (tier: directly observed).
- Income series: 10, 20, 50 and 100 income records (tier: directly observed).
- Coin Collector: eight badges from 1,000 to 1,000,000 coins collected (tier: directly observed).

## Access and eligibility

**O32. Subscription-locked features.**

**Observed:** Fortune City keeps the recording, city and basic reporting features open and holds a set of finance features behind the Sparkful subscription (tier: directly observed). Tapping any of them opens the paywall (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Locked: budget reminders, new budget categories, continuing the expense review past the first few items, categorizing items in the review tab, the month report comparison, the frequency challenge and frequency analysis, records by category and payment type, regular expenses, income, budget and data export in settings (tier: directly observed).
- Locked in the city: subscriber-exclusive themes and Fisher's collection unlock (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O23, O24, O25, O31, O45, O47.*

**O33. Sign-in-locked features.**

**Observed:** Several features require signing in with a Sparkful account: data backup, ranking, visiting friends, and the ads and experiences setting ("Please log into Sparkful to continue") (tier: directly observed). The My Themes screen warns that without logging in, deleting the app permanently deletes all purchases, and offers a sign-in button (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The ranking screen's copy is "Join the great community, develop the city with your friends. Log in with your social account to expand the city with your friends. Then you can show off your rankings and back up all your entries and progress." (tier: directly observed).
- The analyst characterizes the app as heavy on logging in and signing up (tier: directly observed).
- Declining the sign-in hand-off closes it without changing anything (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O5, O39, O40, O47.*

- **Referral.** Fan badges and Best Apps achievements (O41) send the user to install other apps; no reward depends on another person joining.
- **Earning Tasks.** The Best Apps achievements (O41) are part of the achievement catalogue, not a catalogue of compensated tasks disclosing pay and effort.
- **Gifting.** The salesman "gives" diamonds (O20), but he is a product character and no user directs a benefit to another.
- **Record-to-Build Conversion.** Not a library entry, and not to be proposed as one. Turning each logged record into an object in a space the product maintains (O2, O12) is Fortune City's premise, not a mechanic layered on it: the mapping from record to building is what the product is for, in the same way a photo editor's editing and a planner's calendar are what those products are for. The conversion is recorded in plain language in O12. Where such a space carries progression, a collection or a cap, those are classified on their own terms, as here under Leveling, Set Collection and Energy.

# Pass three: proposed new tags

### Refilling Task Board

**Draft definition.** A refilling task board presents a small fixed number of discrete objectives, each with a stated reward, and replaces a completed objective with a new one after a stated wait measured in minutes or hours, independently of any daily or weekly schedule.
**Source observations:** O19, O55
**Conditions it appears to depend on:** A fixed number of slots; objectives short enough to finish within a session; a wait after completion shown on the slot; a skip route.
**Why it is not covered.** Daily / Weekly Quests requires a daily or weekly cadence. Challenge requires an end boundary with an unsuccessful outcome, which the sessions did not show. Minigame requires an outcome that changes nothing outside it.
**Recurrence elsewhere:** Order boards in farming and city-building games, and delivery or request boards that refill on a timer.
**Caveat.** One wait (59 minutes) was observed. The countdowns on other slots were not explained, and whether uncompleted objectives expire was not seen. If Daily / Weekly Quests were widened to any recurring refill interval, this would become a variant of that entry.

### Balance Storage Cap

**Draft definition.** A balance storage cap is a ceiling on how much of a spendable balance the user can hold, displayed beside the balance, raised by upgrading an entity the product maintains, with the product warning the user as the balance approaches the ceiling and directing them to the upgrade.
**Source observations:** O37, O30
**Conditions it appears to depend on:** A balance earned continuously or passively; a displayed cap; an upgrade route that raises it; a warning near the cap.
**Why it is not covered.** Energy constrains actions, not holdings. Piggy Bank holds value in a separate reserve. Leveling classifies the upgrade state, not the ceiling it sets.
**Recurrence elsewhere:** Storage buildings in base-building and city-building games, and idle games whose offline earnings stop at a storage limit.
**Caveat.** Only the warning and the guided upgrade were observed. What happens to coins earned at the cap was not seen, and that behavior would decide whether the cap acts on the user's continued activity.

# Close: system view

The record is the input to everything in the city. Each expense entered (O11) becomes a building (O12), but only up to the daily builder capacity (O13). The number of buildings decides how many citizens can join (O34), citizens work in buildings to produce coins (O15, O16), and coins pay for merges and City Hall levels (O17, O30). Merges and upgrades raise building levels and prosperity (O29, O28), and prosperity and population thresholds fire achievements (O27), each paying three diamonds (O26). Diamonds pay for recruits without envelopes, themes and the Builders Hub (O38), and the Builders Hub raises the daily capacity that limits the conversion of records (O13). The coin storage cap (O37) turns accumulated coins into a finance-level purchase (O30).

Advertisements join the loop at four points (O48): the daily reward (O50), diamonds, the salesman (O20), and merges (O17). A series of achievements counts advertisement views (O26), and any purchase is stated to remove the other advertisements (O46, O49). The tracking permission request is placed at the first rewarded advertisement (O43).

The finance layer depends on the same records. The reporting screens (O22) and the budget (O23) read them, and the expense review (O24) returns them to the user as want and need judgements, then stops at the paywall (O45). The subscription holds most of the analysis (O32), and Fisher, who is presented as the review's helper, is held behind four consecutive days of tracking (O35), which the achievements and collection screens also reward (O53, O31). Return is driven by the daily reward sequence (O50), the nightly reminder (O51), next-day builder capacity, mission refills and work periods (O55), the weekly collection check (O54), and the welcome-back greeting and badges (O56). The rating prompt (O52) is placed on a return visit after a record. Social features, backup and theme protection depend on the Sparkful sign-in (O33, O39, O40), which also connects the app to the rest of the Sparkful family (O41).
