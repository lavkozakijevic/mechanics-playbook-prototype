## Core loop and automation

**O21. Today's plan is a three-part session the user can start immediately.**

**Observed:** The plan screen holds the session for today as an ordered list: a general warm-up, a goblet squat, and a post-workout stretch, with a control to start exercising straight away (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The three parts are presented together before anything is started, so the whole session is visible in advance (tier: directly observed).
- Warm-up and stretch are listed as parts of the session rather than as optional extras (tier: directly observed).
- The screen also carries a plan overview and a control to rate the plan (tier: directly observed).
- A figure showing which muscles the session works sits on this screen, naming adductors, obliques and forearms among others, and naming the machine or movement each belongs to (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O20, O26, O28, O29.*

**O22. Starting the workout runs a guided warm-up of five movements.**

**Observed:** Pressing start workout opens a warm-up that leads the user through neck circles, arm circles, wrist circles, hip circles and ankle circles before the first exercise (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The movements are delivered one at a time and the user marks the warm-up complete before the first exercise opens (tier: directly observed).

*Cross-reference: O21, O23.*

**O23. Marking a set complete opens a rest countdown automatically.**

**Observed:** After the user says an exercise is done, a timer opens by itself and counts down as rest. The user then repeats the exercise, marks it done again, and the countdown opens again for each following set (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- The countdown is opened by the product, not started by the user (tier: directly observed).
- The cycle observed was do the set, mark complete, rest countdown, repeat, across several sets (tier: directly observed).
- A separate timer can also be set by the user on the exercise screen (tier: directly observed).
- The rest duration, and whether it varies by exercise, were not observed (tier: unresolved).

## Economy and resources

No observations. Gymverse maintains no currency, points balance, materials, storage limit or conversion rate anywhere in the session, and no faucet or sink was observed. The calorie and protein targets at O15 are health figures the product calculates and displays, not a quantity the user holds, earns or spends, and nothing in the product consumes or produces them.

## Social

No observations. No other identified person appears anywhere in the session: no other users, no groups, no messaging, no comparison against named others, no transfers between people and no shared state. The two population figures shown during onboarding, two million trainees at O5 and 59 per cent of gym goers at O4, name no individual and place the user against no one. The share routes at O36, O37 and O38 send an image to people outside the product and bring nobody into it. This is a finding: the product is single-player throughout.

# Close: system view

Gymverse spends its entire first act building one object and then charges for it before it has been seen.

Nineteen numbered screens run in front of the product (O1). Most of them feed the plan: the goal at O2, the muscle focus at O6, the location and equipment at O10, the frequency at O11, the scheduled arrangement at O12, the session length at O13 and the imported body details at O14 are all parameters, and the build step at O19 turns them into the calendar at O28 and the session at O21. That is a clean dependency chain, and it is the backbone of the product: everything the user does afterwards is the plan, executed.

A second chain runs alongside it and carries no data at all. The obstacle question at O3 answers each choice with a matching line, but the statistic that follows at O4 is the same whatever was chosen, the experience question at O8 returns the same affirmation to a beginner and to a two-year trainee, and the first-workout question at O16 approves the answer that declines to schedule anything. These screens are inside the numbered count, so they lengthen the setup the user is working through while contributing nothing to the plan being built. The product is doing two jobs in one sequence: collecting parameters, and telling the user it was built for them. Only the first job needs the answers.

The cost is therefore front-loaded and the price arrives at the worst point in it. The paywall opens the moment the build finishes (O39), which is after nineteen questions, a health permission and a set of body measurements, and before a single exercise has been seen. The product appears to know this, because it cancels its own gate as soon as it is refused: pressing the X returns seven days of full access with no payment details (O42). That grant is what made the rest of this record possible, and it is also why Access and eligibility holds one observation (O35). Nothing in this product was locked during the session, so what Pro actually withholds is unknown, and it is one of the two things left unresolved.

What remains once the user is inside is a small, quiet system. The workout runs as a fixed three-part sequence, warm-up, exercise, stretch (O21), and the only automation in it is the rest countdown that opens by itself between sets (O23), which removes the decision to continue at the one point in a session where stopping is easiest. The loop ends in a summary that looks forward twice, to next week's progression and to the next workout (O31). Beyond that there is a weekly weight adjustment shown as a projected graph before any training has happened (O30), counts of workouts and hours (O32), a measurement and photograph record (O34), and an Achievements surface that was named and never opened (O33). Twenty-five library entries were never observed. There is no currency, no capacity limit, no failure state, no unlock and no schedule the user can fall behind on in any way the product was seen to enforce. The plan becomes due; it does not expire.

The social layer is not thin, it is absent. No other person appears anywhere in the product. What exists instead points outward: a composer that turns the product's stored stats into an image (O36), a completion image at the end of each session (O37), and a total activity figure with a route out (O38). The composer is bound to the screenshot gesture, so the moment the user reaches for the artifact they were going to make themselves, the product substitutes its own branded version. That is the same direction the rating ask at O46 runs in, and both of them fire before the record they depend on exists: the share composer opened before a single workout, and the five-star ask arrived before the app had been used at all. The product asks for advocacy in advance of giving anyone a reason for it.

Return machinery is correspondingly early and correspondingly light. Notification permission is taken at step eighteen, with a preview of the notification and a heading written as loss prevention (O45), and a notification arrived the same day, while the user was still inside the app, in wording that matches the training-history answer given at step nine (O47). Workout reminders sit in settings (O48) and the calendar decides when they would have something to say (O28). That is the whole of it: no event, no countdown, no expiry, no streak, nothing accumulating that a missed day would cost. The product's continuity language is everywhere, at O3, O11, O45 and O47, and the continuity mechanic is nowhere. If a user stops training, nothing in the record shows Gymverse holding anything they would lose by stopping, which is the sharpest structural finding in this session and the one most worth testing against a longer walkthrough.
