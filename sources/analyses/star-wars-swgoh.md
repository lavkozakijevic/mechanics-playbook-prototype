## Core loop and automation

**O12. Turn-based squad battles.**

**Observed:** Battles are turn-based, and the guide states that units take turns based on their speed, with a blue speed bar under each health bar showing how close a unit is to its next turn (tier: directly observed). A turn order tracker shows who acts next and can be hidden by tapping; the guide notes that units gaining bonus turn meter can jump the line (tier: directly observed). Each character has basic and special abilities with cooldowns, plus passive qualities (tier: directly observed). Beneficial effects appear as green badges and harmful effects as red badges, and effects seen include Taunt, healing immunity, buff immunity, ability block, offense up, potency up, critical hit immunity, advantage and foresight (tier: directly observed). Battles run in waves ending in a final encounter, and enemies are seen healing, dodging and taunting (tier: directly observed). Victories are graded in stars (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Stats shown on a character include strength, agility and tactics with growth values, health, protection, speed, critical damage, potency, tenacity, health steal, defense penetration, and separate physical and special offense and survivability values (tier: directly observed).
- The narrator reports no lost battle in the recorded sessions (tier: directly observed).
- In the challenge battles the narrator deliberately fields the weakest characters and reports the fight taking noticeably longer (tier: directly observed).

**O23. Four upgrade tracks per character.**

Relic amplifiers appear on every character and are locked until gear tier 13 (tier: directly observed).

**Detail:**
- Observed costs: training 106 credits early on, training Anakin to level 19 for 11,000 credits, activation 100 credits, promotion to three stars 20,000 credits, Anakin's promotion 10,000 credits, one ability upgrade for Obi-Wan 100,000 credits and another 10,000 credits (tier: directly observed).

The only acquisition route shown is the Episode Track, on an unstated lane, so neither a paid path nor a repeatable non-paid path is established and the parent label is used (O26, O39) (tier: directly observed).
**Variants present:** Action tokens (O39).
**Variants not established:** Earned credits, purchased credits, expiring credits and restricted credits.
**Alternative considered:** Hard Currency and Soft Currency were considered and cannot be applied until the acquisition path is shown. Boosters does not apply, because tokens are applied on the upgrade screen rather than inside a battle.

## Entries considered and not applied

- Companion: the entry's invariant requires held entities that act alongside the user's primary representation, and in Galaxy of Heroes the squad is the entirety of what acts in battle, so there is nothing for the characters to act alongside (O12, O13, O21). Reading the condition as satisfied by the player's account and player level existing separately (O8, O22) would make it vacuous, since an account always exists separately, and would apply the entry to every unit in every squad game. In plain language: the player holds a persistent roster of characters, acquired through quests, a free data card, shards, Bronzium draws, shipments and paid offers (O9, O11, O38, O41, O42, O53); up to five of the player's own characters fill the squad for a battle, with a sixth slot reserved for a borrowed unit from level 18 (O21, O45); the characters are the only actors in battle, taking turns by speed and using their own abilities under the player's direction or on auto (O12, O13); and each character carries its own level, star rank, gear tier and ability levels (O23). What would change the call is a squad game in which something the user directs acts alongside the roster rather than being composed of it. The Companion entry may need an amendment for squad games with no avatar.
- Streak: the April login rewards count days logged in and state that missed rewards are lost, but a missed day does not reset or alter the sequence (O64).
- First-Purchase Bonus: starter deals and bundles carry purchase limits, but no benefit tied to a first purchase within a scope was observed (O53, O58).
- Purchase Ladder: every offer seen can be bought directly, and no later offer was shown locked behind an earlier purchase (O53, O54, O59).

- Season Content Pass: the Episode Pass opens a reward lane and exclusive quests, not a bundle of content releases (O61).
- Referral: the Allies invite by email shows no reward to the inviter tied to anything the invitee does (O44, O52).
- Gifting: the store's Gifts tab is only a locked label, and the gift packs handed out are system grants with no sender (O11, O54); the locked tab is carried under Unresolved.
- Group Membership: the ally list is a set of pairwise relationships, borrowing works without being allies, and no effect of ally status on product behavior was observed (O44, O45, O46).
- Community Space: group chats are mentioned without any recorded participation, and the forums open outside the product (O47, O51).

- Leaderboard: no ordered list of players was observed, and the competitive surfaces are locked (O49); the locked arena is carried under Unresolved.
- Boosters: sim tickets obtain a completed battle outside any battle instance, and no consumable item applied inside a battle was observed (O17).

- Variable Reward Schedule: free Bronzium draws follow a visible countdown and every draw pays a reward (O41).
- Source Finder (a proposal from this analysis, rejected on scope; not a library entry): the Find control that routes a user from a stated need to the battles that drop an item, and names other characters competing for it, is navigation rather than a designed experience pointing the user toward a behavior (O23, O38). The same control also lists paid bundles and packs for characters, which is the presentation of offers and falls outside a tag on its own terms (O62). A control that lists where an item or entity can be obtained should not be proposed as a tag in future analyses.

# Pass three: proposed new tags

### Borrowed Unit

**Draft definition.** A borrowed unit is a persistent entity held by another identified user that the product offers to the current user for a single activity instance, placed in a position reserved for such units alongside the user's own entities, whose use pays the borrower a stated reward, and after which the product may prompt the borrower to form a standing relationship with the lender.

**Source observations:** O45, O46, O36, O44.

**Conditions it appears to depend on:** A roster of persistent acting entities held by many users, so that other users' entities are available to offer (O28, O45). A reserved position that does not displace the user's own entities (O45). A reward paid in a currency with its own sink (O36, O41). A relationship system into which the borrower can be invited after use (O44, O46).

**Why it is not covered.** Companion excludes entities that are another person's representation. Gifting requires a sender to initiate a grant, and the lender here takes no observed action. Referral requires an introduction followed by a completion event. Group Membership and Clan / Guild concern groups rather than a one-instance loan. Soft Currency covers the ally points earned but not the lending surface that produces them.

**Recurrence elsewhere:** Observed in this app only. No other app's sessions were consulted in this run, so recurrence is expected rather than observed.

**Caveat.** The proposal remains open. It rests on one app, and the lender's side was never observed: whether lenders are rewarded or notified, how offered units are selected, and whether allies' units are offered differently from strangers' were all unseen. The recorded offers came from players inactive for thousands of days (O46), which may reflect this account's level band rather than the general rule. Either of two observations would settle it: a second app carrying the same shape, a unit held by another identified user placed in a reserved position for one activity instance with a stated reward to the borrower; or a session in this app showing what the lender receives when a unit is borrowed and whether the lender is notified.

### Directed Objective List

**Draft definition.** A directed objective list is a persistent, non-recurring set of product-defined objectives shown a few at a time, in which each objective carries a control that takes the user directly to the place where it is completed, and completing one releases the next.

**Source observations:** O5, O25.

**Conditions it appears to depend on:** A product with many surfaces and locked features, where the next useful action is not obvious (O4, O30). A guide or copy that establishes the list as the answer to what to do next (O5).

**Why it is not covered.** Daily / Weekly Quests requires a daily or weekly issuance or refresh rule, which journey quests do not show. Challenge requires an end boundary. Achievement records attained criteria after the fact. Milestone recognizes thresholds in a measure rather than issuing objectives.

**Recurrence elsewhere:** No other app's sessions were consulted in this run, so recurrence is expected rather than observed.

**Caveat.** The proposal remains open. Its distinction from Daily / Weekly Quests holds, since nothing in the list is issued or refreshed on a period (O25). Whether the list ever runs out, expires or follows a fixed order was not observed, and that decides whether this is a mechanic or an onboarding checklist that ends once the first hours of guidance are over. A session showing the journey quest list at a later stage of play, either still issuing objectives or exhausted, would settle it. The same list surface also carries daily, episode and character quests, which are tagged on their own terms.

# Close: system view

This section describes how the observed pieces connect. It is interpretation of the record above, and each claim carries its tier.

**Battles feed everything else.** Each battle consumes energy (O15) and pays player XP, credits, shards, gear and sim tickets (O3, O17, O22, O38) (tier: directly observed). Player XP moves the player level (O22), and the player level opens the hub tables, modes and store tabs one at a time (O30, O31) (tier: directly observed). The level-up screen also raises the energy maximum and grants bonus energy, so advancing through battles funds further battles (O15, O22) (tier: directly observed). The narrator's energy readings of 158 against a maximum of 68 show the early surplus this produces (O15) (tier: directly observed).

**Characters are the unit every system acts on.** Credits, training droids, shards, gear and ability materials all flow into the four character tracks (O23, O35, O38) (tier: directly observed). Stronger characters make campaign battles easy enough to run on auto (O13) (tier: strongly supported, based on the narrator's easy wins after upgrading). They also meet requirements elsewhere: dark side characters for Dark Side battles, character level 50 for Mod Battles, seven stars for the Shard Store, gear tier 13 for relic amplifiers and required units for challenge tiers (O20, O31, O32) (tier: directly observed). The roster's missing members and shard counts point back to hard mode, shipments and draws (O16, O28, O41, O42) (tier: directly observed).

**Hard mode and sim tickets are the shard route.** Hard mode is named as the best shard source and is limited to five completions a day per battle (O10, O16) (tier: directly observed). Sim tickets let three-starred battles be repeated instantly, but each simulation still costs energy, which ties the shard route back to the energy pools (O15, O17) (tier: directly observed).

**Quests direct activity into the economy.** Daily quests ask for energy use, battles, a data card opening and shipment purchases, and completing them opens a prize box of currencies and materials (O18) (tier: directly observed). Episode and character quests pay episode points into the Episode Track (O19, O26), whose rewards include lightspeed tokens that upgrade characters (O39) (tier: directly observed). Some episode quest rewards are held for Episode Pass holders, which connects the quest list to the pass purchase (O33, O61) (tier: directly observed). Journey quests and their Go buttons sequence the first hours and point into each newly opened feature (O5, O25) (tier: directly observed).

**Other players enter through borrowing.** From level 18 a borrowed unit fills a sixth slot in every battle and pays ally points (O45), ally points buy Bronzium draws and weekly shipment items (O36, O41), and each borrowing ends with a prompt to send an ally request (O46) (tier: directly observed). The recorded requests all went to players inactive for years (O46) (tier: directly observed). Whether this routing is designed to reach lapsed players or is a side effect of unfiltered suggestions is not established (tier: plausible, both readings remain open). Guilds and arenas, which appear on the hub, in shipment tabs and on profiles, stayed locked throughout, so the social system beyond borrowing was not observed (O48, O49) (tier: directly observed).

**Purchases shortcut the same tracks.** Starter deals, character bundles, calendars and the Hyperdrive Bundle sell characters, credits, droids, materials and energy, the same inputs battles provide (O53, O54, O58, O59, O60) (tier: directly observed). Crystals, sold in packages, buy Chromium packs, credit packs, energy refills and shipments (O34, O55, O56, O57) (tier: directly observed). Find on a character lists paid bundles beside the activities that drop its shards (O62) (tier: directly observed). Offers appear on nearly every return to the hub during the first session, often without a price until the payment step (O53, O58) (tier: directly observed).

**Return is organized on several clocks at once.** The April login calendar runs by days logged in within the month (O64), calendars run over 30 days (O60), daily quests reset each day (O18), episode quests add one a day (O19), featured shipments refresh every few hours and episode shipments weekly (O42), and free Bronzium draws return about every ten minutes (O41) (tier: directly observed). Red badges mark each of these when something is waiting (O66), and the app requests notification permission on the second launch (O65) (tier: directly observed). Era launches, the events board, a film tie-in icon and loading screen, changed cantina dialogue and a free character offer arrive together on a return a month later (O67, O68, O69, O70) (tier: directly observed).

**First run sets the frame for all of this.** The first launch shows the whole hub with nearly every table padlocked (O4), which is the same map of gates that the player level later opens (O30) (tier: directly observed). Setup is postponed: account import is optional, age and name are asked after two battles, and asset downloads start mid-play (O2, O7, O8) (tier: directly observed). The first paid offer follows the name step directly (O8, O53) (tier: directly observed).
