**Source.** This analysis is built from two Granola walkthrough sessions, retrieved as full transcripts. The first, "Subway Surfers analysis (half-done)" (07 May 2026), runs from first launch through the age and tracking prompts, the guided first run, the first return to the home screen and its rewarded offers, and a first pass over the character, board, upgrade and collection surfaces. The second, "Subway Surfers ad strategies" (14 May 2026), is a focused pass over the shop and its three tabs, the advertisement-related offers and ad tickets, the currency packs, the daily rewards, daily login and catch-up surfaces, the Coins Galore and Bonus Keys Trail offers, Top Run, adding friends, the Me profile and Surfer Studio, the characters, boards, upgrades and collections tabs, and the missions, achievements and quests surfaces. Both transcripts are machine speech-to-text of live narration by one speaker with no typed notes. Numbers, prices and names are reported as transcribed; where the transcription is fragmented or internally inconsistent, that is marked at the relevant observation. Observations taken from the second session describe the app as it stood on 14 May 2026, a week after the first; each observation states which session it comes from. The app version is never stated in either session and is not inferred.

- **Spendable Credits and Tokens.** Coins, keys and ad tickets satisfy it, but Soft Currency and Hard Currency are its specializations and are reported instead, with this entry noted as the parent (O22, O23, O26).

- **Challenge.** Top Run's weekly entry score is a threshold to join a comparison rather than a bounded objective whose completion changes the system response on its own (O20, O32).
- **Set Collection.** Characters unlocked by collecting hats, tape recorders, guitars and flying saucers may form a tracked set, but the session does not establish whether the requirement is a set of distinct items or a count of one type, or how member state is shown (O15).

# Close: system view

The run (O6) is the single activity everything else feeds from and back into. It produces coins and a score; coins accumulate as a balance (O22), and the score is scaled by the score multiplier (O12). The multiplier is advanced by missions (O9), which are completed by playing runs or bought out with coins or an advertisement (O9). The multiplier in turn gates Quests and Collections (O17, O18), and the locked surfaces offer a paid bundle that adds five multiplier levels (O45), so the progression spine can be advanced by play, by coins, by advertisements or by money.

Coins flow out into boosters (O10, O11), permanent upgrades (O13), characters and boards (O42) and mission buyouts (O9). Boosters and upgrades feed back into the run, raising score and coin collection, which closes the loop between the economy and the core activity. Keys (O23) sit beside coins as a second currency that buys characters, flash-deal items and missed login days (O47), and the Bonus Keys Trail (O44) turns a single key purchase into ten days of returns.

Advertisements are the connective tissue. They substitute for coin prices on boosters (O10, O11), increase the post-run prize (O28), complete missions (O9), recover missed login days (O47), fill the Daily Rewards slots (O48), count toward Mystery Box Mania (O30) and pay the timed Coins Galore offer (O49). Ad tickets (O26, O39) and the Skip Ads, No More Forced Ads and Skip 30 Ads offers (O36, O37, O38) are sold against that same advertisement layer, and the Boombot banner (O35) attaches the removal of pop-up advertisements to any purchase. The store's leading position is given to these advertisement-related offers (O36).

The return surfaces presented on opening the app (O46, O48, O49) depend on that advertisement layer and on the currencies: Daily Login points to a 20-login board that is also held behind a gate in the board catalogue (O19), its missed days are recovered with keys or advertisements (O47), and Daily Rewards and Coins Galore pay only for advertisements under a countdown. Social surfaces are thin and reward-linked: adding a friend pays coins and a character (O31), and Top Run compares the user with friends and country players weekly behind a score threshold (O20, O32). Characters and boards (O42), bought with money, currencies or earned through collecting, events, friends and logins, are where most of these separate routes converge.

What the record cannot show is how the gated surfaces behave (O16, O17, O18), whether characters and boards affect the run (O42), and how any of these loops change over repeated days; the sessions are two short walkthroughs a week apart.
