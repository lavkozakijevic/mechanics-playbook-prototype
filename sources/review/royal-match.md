## Mechanics

### Lives

**Implementation summary:** Royal Match charges a heart for failing a level, not for starting one, and offers a timer, coins or teammates to win it back.

**How it works.** Hearts come into view when a level goes wrong. You start with 5, and Royal Match takes one only when you fail a level, never for starting a level or trying it again, so a run of clears leaves the balance untouched. The first failures arrive from level 18, and from then on a missing heart is a decision about how to get back in. Each spent heart returns on a timer, and the lives panel shows how long is left. You can skip the wait by paying 900 gold coins for a refill, or by asking your teammates, which is where the free lives option sends you when it has nothing for you. The same fork appears one step earlier: when you run out of moves, the game offers 5 more for the same 900 coins. Paid offers sell time instead of hearts, periods of infinite hearts from 15 minutes up to 18 hours, and the Easter Pass raises how many hearts you can hold from 5 to 8. The motivation is fear of losing something, and the game answers it 3 ways: a clock, a price and a teammate.

**Illustration brief.** The one idea: a missing heart has 3 exits. The lives panel with 3 hearts held and a countdown of 7 minutes 30 seconds to the next one, beside the 900-coin refill and the request to teammates.

**What stands out.** A heart is taken for failing, not for trying, so the cost arrives only after a loss. What the paid offers sell is time: infinite hearts for 15 minutes up to 18 hours, in place of heart counts.

**Trigger:** Failing a level.

**What it needs:** A balance of up to 5 hearts, with a timer running for each one spent.

**How it connects:** Teammates send hearts through Gifting, the coins for a refill come from Soft Currency, the offers in Hard Currency sell periods of infinite hearts, and the Easter Pass in Seasonal Progression Pass raises the limit to 8.

**Worth noticing:** Royal Match prices a heart refill and 5 extra moves at the same 900 coins.

**Screenshots needed:** The lives panel at 3 hearts with its countdown; the extra-moves offer when a level is lost.

### Boosters

**Implementation summary:** Royal Match introduces its assist items one at a time, each as it becomes usable, and later sells them by the hour.

**How it works.** You first meet boosters at level 2, as a row of items you can see but not tap. Each one arrives later with a short lesson at the level where you can first use it, the royal hammer after 6 or 7 games, an arrow after level 14, a cannon after level 16 and a jester's hat after level 18, and each does something different to the board: clearing any one object, a row, a column, or shuffling everything. Every item is a counted holding. You pick one before a level starts or use it in the middle of one, and the count falls with each use. That gives you a second option on a level you're about to fail, and it makes the stock something you can spend to protect what you hold: at level 36 a power-up is spent to avoid failing and losing the Butler's Gift. Supplies come from chests, events, the Easter Pass, a reward ladder and offers, and the shop sells them by the hour, from 1 hour to 100 hours of unlimited use. The motivation is curiosity at first, since you can see the items before you can use them, and then fear of losing something.

**Illustration brief.** The one idea: the items are visible before they're usable. The locked booster row at level 2, beside the royal hammer's unlock lesson and an offer card showing endless boosters for 1 hour.

**What stands out.** You see the boosters at level 2, locked, long before you can use any, and each is taught at the level where it unlocks. Most of what the named treasure bundles and the team offers contain is booster time rather than booster counts.

**Trigger:** Tapping an item before a level or during one.

**What it needs:** A held quantity of the item, or an active unlimited period, and a level at which it has unlocked.

**How it connects:** Progression Gate releases each item at its level, Streak's Butler's Gift starts a level with power-ups already in hand, and Hard Currency, Seasonal Progression Pass and Purchase Ladder all hand out or sell boosters.

**Worth noticing:** Royal Match sells booster use by the hour as well as by the item.

**Screenshots needed:** The locked booster row at level 2; the royal hammer unlock lesson; an offer showing endless boosters.

### Challenge

**Implementation summary:** Royal Match gives every level a target and a move limit with no timer, and changes the target as the levels go on.

**How it works.** Every level asks the same of you: reach the target before your moves run out. There's no clock, so the pressure is a number counting down, and the king's face follows it, looking worried when about 3 moves remain with work left to do. What the target is keeps changing, breaking a box, collecting 36 wooden pieces, matching next to mailboxes, matching next to royal eggs, so the same board keeps asking a new question. Level 39 is flagged as a hard level before you play it. Now and then the play button gives way to King's Nightmare, a short activity with its own goal and a 50-coin reward that you can play or skip, such as carrying water to a sprinkler in 20 moves. Events run on top of the levels, Propeller Madness, the Egg Hunt and the team tournament, and each one states its goal and shows how long is left. The motivation is progress, with the countdowns on events adding a deadline.

**Illustration brief.** The one idea: a level is a target against a shrinking number of moves. A level start showing its target and moves left, beside the King's Nightmare button standing in for the numbered level.

**What stands out.** What limits you on a level is its move count, not a clock; the one timed activity is a King's Nightmare. The hard level is labelled before you play it, and every King's Nightmare can be skipped.

**Trigger:** Tapping the play button, which now and then offers King's Nightmare in its place.

**What it needs:** A level with a stated target and a number of moves.

**How it connects:** Failing a level spends a heart in Lives, Boosters help you through, events advance from your cleared levels in Experience Points, and the hard level carries a bigger reward in Reward Multiplier.

**Worth noticing:** Royal Match swaps the numbered level for a separate short activity from time to time, each with a goal of its own and a reward you can decline.

**Screenshots needed:** A level with its target and move counter; the King's Nightmare button; the hard-level flag at level 39.

### Leveling

**Implementation summary:** Royal Match keeps one number that rises with each clear, shows it on the home screen and leaderboards, and unlocks features at set values.

**How it works.** Your player level is the number that follows you through the whole game. It starts at 1, goes up by 1 each time you clear a level, and sits on the home screen beside the area you're building. It's also the number other players see: the leaderboards list levels next to names and pictures, and the world list reaches 13,401. Teams and the collection both ask for a level before they open, so the same number serves as your progress and as your standing. The tournament adds a second, separate track of its own, an armour that changes from silver to gold. The motivation is progress, with status added once other players can see the number.

**Illustration brief.** The one idea: one number, seen 2 ways. The home screen's level beside a leaderboard row showing a level in the thousands, with the tournament armour in silver and gold at one side.

**What stands out.** The same number is your private progress and your public standing. The tournament runs its own track beside it, with an armour colour that has nothing to do with your player level.

**Trigger:** Clearing a level.

**What it needs:** Levels cleared, one at a time.

**How it connects:** Progression Gate reads from the number, Leaderboard lists it, Comparative Rank carries the tournament armour, and Milestone carries the tournament's knight title.

**Worth noticing:** Royal Match gives the tournament its own armour track, kept apart from your player level.

**Screenshots needed:** The home screen showing level and area; the players leaderboard with levels beside names; the tournament armour changing colour.

### Progression Gate

**Implementation summary:** Royal Match lets you see what's locked, from untappable items to a browsable team, and ties each unlock to a player level.

**How it works.** You find the gates by walking into them. At level 2 a row of boosters is on screen and none of it can be tapped. Later the teams item tells you to reach level 21, and you can still open a team to read its description, capacity, score and the level it asks for, but tapping join sends you back to level 21. The game prompts you to join at level 20, one level early, with free lives and free rewards as the stated reason. The collection is the barest lock: it names level 41 and describes nothing else. Assist items are the gentlest, each released at a level of its own with a short lesson where you first use it. Every lock names a level you can reach only by playing more, so the next unlock is something to play toward, and the item gates change what each level can be played with. The locks draw on curiosity and progress: you can see that something is there before you can use it.

**Illustration brief.** The one idea: 3 locks, each showing a different amount. The team join message beside the collection's level 41 lock and the locked booster row.

**What stands out.** Each gate shows something different: a team in full, a bare level number, a visible tool you can't tap. The join prompt arrives a level before you can act on it.

**Trigger:** Reaching a stated player level, or tapping a locked team, tab or item.

**What it needs:** A player level, which rises by 1 for every level you clear.

**How it connects:** The gates read from your level in Leveling, and they hold back Clan / Guild, Set Collection and Boosters.

**Worth noticing:** Royal Match shows the whole team screen before level 21 and puts only a level number on the collection.

**Screenshots needed:** The level 21 join message; the collection's lock; the locked booster row.

### Soft Currency

**Implementation summary:** Royal Match runs 2 earned balances: stars that pay only for castle tasks, and coins that pay for refills and extra moves.

**How it works.** You earn both balances by playing and spend them in different places. A cleared level pays 1 star, and stars buy one thing, the next piece of your castle. Tasks cost 1 star at first and 2 later in the first area, and you can let stars pile up and build when you like, with a marker by the area chest reminding you that tasks are waiting. Tapping a task you can't yet afford tells you to beat levels to earn stars. Coins come from levels, King's Nightmare, bonus levels, chests, event rewards and even from sending a teammate a life, which pays 5. They go to the moments a level goes wrong: 900 for a heart refill and 900 for 5 more moves. Stars give your effort a visible place to go, which is progress. Coins are what a failure costs, which is fear of losing something.

**Illustration brief.** The one idea: 2 balances with separate sources and separate uses. The home screen with its star, coin and heart balances, and the shortage message on the fountain task.

**What stands out.** Stars have one source and one use; coins have many sources and are spent where a level fails. Undoing a failure costs 900 coins, and a whole bonus level pays more than 500.

**Trigger:** Clearing a level, which pays a star; failing a level or running out of moves, which is where coins get spent.

**What it needs:** Cleared levels for stars; play, chests, events and teammates for coins.

**How it connects:** Hard Currency sells the same coins, Milestone pays them out in chests at area completion, Gifting pays 5 coins for a sent life, and Lives and Challenge are where they are spent.

**Worth noticing:** Royal Match accepts stars only as payment for castle tasks.

**Screenshots needed:** The home screen balances; the star shortage message; the area task list with its costs.

### Hard Currency

**Implementation summary:** Royal Match sells the same coins you earn in play, and bundles them with time-limited unlimited boosters and hearts.

**How it works.** You reach the shop by tapping your coin balance, and what it sells is the coin you've been earning. Coins pay for the moments a level goes wrong. The plain packs are priced as a ladder where more money buys more coins per unit: 1,000 coins at 1.99 at the bottom, 100,000 at 99.99 at the top. The bundles go further than coins. The special offer at 1.99 adds an hour of endless boosters and a set of power-ups to 2,000 coins, and the named treasures stretch the time from 12 hours of endless boosters in queen's treasure at 19.99 to 100 hours of boosters and 18 hours of hearts in superior treasure at 99.99. Queen's treasure carries a popular banner and superior treasure a best value one. What you're buying is room to keep going after a failed level, and the bundles pair coins with time rather than with quantities alone. The motivation is the same fear of losing something that hearts draw on, with the shop selling the way out.

**Illustration brief.** The one idea: the coin you earn is the coin you buy. The shop's first view with the special offer and princess treasure above the coin packs, beside the named bundles with their popular and best value banners.

**What stands out.** One balance has 2 ways in, play and purchase. Every bundle above the cheapest pairs coins with time-limited unlimited items rather than with quantities alone.

**Trigger:** Tapping your coin balance.

**What it needs:** A coin balance for the purchase to land in.

**How it connects:** The coins are the balance from Soft Currency, the booster and heart periods come from Boosters and Lives, and the team offers sit in Gifting.

**Worth noticing:** Royal Match leads its shop with bundles that include time-limited unlimited items, ahead of the plain coin packs.

**Screenshots needed:** The shop's first view; the more offers screen with the named treasures; the extended coin pack list.

### Milestone

**Implementation summary:** Royal Match ends each castle area with a chest and a celebration, and sets a bonus level at 20 and 40.

**How it works.** Finishing an area's last task is the biggest moment in the building loop. You get a chest to open, a celebration in which the king is pleased, a look at the whole finished area, and a tap to unlock the next one. The chest is a payout as well as a landmark: the area 1 chest held 250 coins and 4 kinds of boosters and power-ups, and the area 3 chest 250 coins, further items and 4 cards for the Culinary Collection. Area 1 completes at level 11, area 2 at level 21 and area 3 shortly after level 41, in a map of 152. Bonus levels at 20 and 40 mark a different kind of landmark: no pass or fail, just as many coins as you can gather in the moves you're given, 20 of them with 2 TNTs to start. The tournament marks its own, a knight title once you've contributed enough tokens. The motivation is progress made visible: a point you can reach, and a moment that says you did.

**Illustration brief.** The one idea: a finish line that pays. The area completion chest opening, beside the level 20 bonus level with its pile of gold and its move counter.

**What stands out.** An area ends with a chest, a celebration and a view of the finished area before the next one opens. Bonus levels have no pass or fail, only the amount you collect.

### Experience Points

## Onboarding and first run

Royal Match opens with 2 permission steps and then goes straight into a level. Within the first 2 levels you've also built your first pieces of castle, and the home screen is left for you to explore.

### O1. Tracking comes first

Royal Match opens in portrait orientation, and the first thing it does is ask whether it can track your activity across other companies' apps and websites. The request comes before any gameplay, any account step and any explanation of the game, and notifications aren't asked about at this point.

### O2. Agreeing to the policy

After the tracking request, Royal Match asks you to agree to its privacy policy, and you have to agree before you continue. There's no account creation, sign-in or identity check before you play.

### O3. A guided first match

Royal Match puts you straight into a level and tells you to make a match next to a box to break it, so the match-three action is taught on the board itself. As soon as the first match is made, it tells you the lesson is completed. The instruction points at a particular box rather than describing the action in general.

### O4. Pointing out tasks

Once the first lesson is done, Royal Match tells you the task list can be opened and shows you where to find it.

### O5. The first castle task

Royal Match then tells you to build a castle. It shows your progress through the area and says the whole thing takes 6 steps. The first step costs 1 star, which you already hold, so you spend it and the castle appears before you've cleared another level. The progress indicator first shows at the moment of this build, and the castle is drawn in the style of a fairy-tale castle.

### O6. Short of stars

The next task is a fountain, also 1 star. Tapping it tells you that you don't have enough stars, and that beating levels is how you earn them. The route to more stars is given at the moment you run short.

### O7. Locked boosters, level 2

Level 2 asks you to collect 36 wooden pieces. A row of boosters sits on screen, all locked and impossible to tap, and you can start the level without making any choice.

### O8. Costs start rising

After the level, Royal Match prompts you to improve the castle again and plays an animation as the fountain is built. The next task, installing gazebos, costs 2 stars, double the first 2. Each build is followed straight away by the next task rather than by a return to the board.

### O9. Your first home screen

After 2 levels and 2 builds, you reach the home screen with the castle on it and can look around freely. A grey and white profile icon sits at the top left and a settings button at the top right, and 3 balances show 2,093 gold coins, 5 hearts and gold stars. You also see your current level and area, level 3 and area 1. A bar of 5 items runs along the bottom: a book with a bookmark and stars, a golden cup, a home button, a badge showing several people and a set of cards.

## Core loop and automation

Every level in Royal Match is a target with a number of moves, and a failed one can be played again. Around that, the game adds assist items, short interludes, bonus levels and, from level 27, events that run on the same levels.

### O10. Target and moves

Each level tells you what to achieve and how many moves you have left, and it carries no timer. Reach the target within the moves and the level is complete; run out of moves and it ends as a failure, which you can start again. Completing a level gives you 1 star.

### O11. Targets keep changing

What you have to destroy or collect changes as the levels go on: breaking a box, collecting 36 wooden pieces, destroying patches of grass, matching next to mailboxes to collect mail, and matching next to royal eggs to break them. Mailboxes aren't destroyed. Each qualifying match collects mail from the mailboxes beside it, so playing next to them is what counts. The hard level at 39 is made of 3 mailboxes.

### O12. When failure arrives

Difficulty rises gradually. Levels up to 10 can be cleared without a booster, and boosters stay in stock rather than being spent. The first failures come from level 18: one level is failed once, level 19 is failed twice in a row, and the game gets much harder after level 21.

### O13. Hearts and their timer

You start with 5 hearts and lose one each time you fail a level. A spent heart takes 24 to 25 minutes to come back, and with 3 hearts held, Royal Match shows the next one arriving in 7 minutes 30 seconds. In the lives panel Royal Match offers a refill for 900 gold coins, and a free lives option that opens a view showing none in your inbox, with a prompt to ask your teammates and a button to send the request.

### O14. Paying to keep going

The first time you run out of moves, Royal Match offers 5 more to keep playing for 900 gold coins, the same price as a heart refill. You can decline, and the level is recorded as a failure that you can retry. A whole bonus level pays more than 500 coins.

### O15. Assist items unlock

Royal Match releases its assist items one at a time and teaches each at the level where you can first use it. After 6 or 7 games the royal hammer arrives, 3 of them, able to clear any object on the board, from the next level on, and you're told to tap the hammer and then the item to clear. At that point 3 more items are still locked. The arrow, which clears a row, unlocks after level 14, the cannon, which clears a column, after level 16, and the jester's hat, which shuffles the board, after level 18, with 5 left after you use one. TNT arrives from offers and from the Easter Pass, and as a starting item in bonus levels, and Propeller Madness names the propeller, which erases a row or a column. Each item's count is shown and falls as you use it.

### O16. Choosing before play

Before a level starts, Royal Match shows a booster selection, which at level 2 is there but entirely locked. Once items have unlocked, you can pick them before a level or use them during it, and you can start a level without picking any. At level 36 a power-up is spent to avoid failing a level and losing the Butler's Gift state.

### O17. King's Nightmare

At intervals the play button gives way to King's Nightmare, a short activity with a 50-coin reward that you can play or skip. Each one has its own goal: carrying water to a sprinkler in 20 moves, breaking all the boxes before time runs out, and later getting water to a king you can't see, in 30 moves. The first takes about 6 moves. The second runs on a clock rather than a move count, and the third carries a doubled reward marker and hides its target. Each ends with the king thanking you, confetti and trumpets, and after the first, the button goes back to the numbered level.

### O18. Bonus levels

Level 20 is a bonus level, shown with a pile of gold and a crown, and it tells you to collect as many coins as possible in the moves you're given, 20 of them, with 2 TNTs to start. Level 40 is a bonus level of the same kind. There's no pass or fail; the result is how much you collect. Matches next to coins collect them, and combining the 2 TNTs gives 80 coins at once. A whole bonus level pays more than 500 coins.

### O19. A hard level, flagged

Royal Match marks level 39 as a hard level before you enter it. Passing it triples the reward and gives 30 points toward the Egg Hunt. The level is made of 3 mailboxes to collect mail from.

### O20. The worried king

When you're down to around 3 moves with work left to do, the king looks worried. When you succeed, he thanks you with confetti. His expression follows the state of the level; it isn't something you choose or own.

### O21. Butler's Gift

Royal Match introduces Butler's Gift with the rule to beat levels on your first try to start the next level with power-ups. Later it shows the gift as active on 2 out of 3. At level 36 a power-up is spent to avoid failing a level and losing the gift.

### O22. A 15-minute window

At level 33 a doubled-reward marker appears beside the level and a 15-minute countdown starts, with rewards doubled for that time. What you give your team and what you earn toward Propeller Madness both double while it runs. Royal Match explains the marker when you open it.

### O23. Spending stars, or not

Each time you hold enough stars, Royal Match prompts you to spend them on the current area task, and you can dismiss the prompt. You can keep playing and let stars pile up without building, and a marker beside the area chest shows that tasks are waiting. Area 1's tasks are the castle, the fountain, gazebos, bushes, swans and flowers, 6 steps in all. Area 2 includes installing glass, laying the carpet and placing chairs, and area 3, the dining room, has 9 things to customise. Costs rise from 1 star to 2 within area 1, and a build completes the moment the stars are spent, with an animation.

### O24. Finishing an area

Completing an area gives you a chest to open, a celebration in which the king is pleased, a view of the finished area, and then a new area to unlock with a tap. The area 1 chest pays 250 gold coins and 4 kinds of boosters and power-ups, and the area 3 chest pays 250 coins, further items and 4 cards for the Culinary Collection. Area 1 completes at level 11, area 2 at level 21 and area 3 shortly after level 41.

### O25. Events ride on levels

Once its events are running, Royal Match moves them forward through the same match-three levels you're already playing. Tournament tokens, Easter Pass keys, Egg Hunt eggs and Propeller Madness steps all advance this way. The Easter Pass tells you to beat levels and collect keys to unlock rewards, and the Egg Hunt tells you to beat levels and collect eggs to smash the next basket. After a completed level, a token moves into the team tournament.

## Goals and progression

Royal Match keeps a player level that rises with every cleared level, a map of 152 areas to fill and a profile of running stats. From level 27 it adds timed events, one at a time, each moved forward by the same levels.

### O26. Your player level

Royal Match keeps a current level for you, shows it on the home screen, raises it by 1 for each level you clear and uses it as the condition for other parts of the game. After 2 cleared levels the home screen reads level 3. Other players' levels appear on the leaderboards, the highest at 13,401. Teams and the collection both name a level as their condition.

### O27. 152 areas

Tapping the first item in the navigation bar opens the map of areas, which shows the areas you hold and those still to come, and gives the total as 152. You start outside, and the map scrolls upward through the rest. The first areas, in order, are area 1, the throne room, the dining room and the garden.

### O28. Your profile stats

Your profile shows your level, whether you're in a team and when you joined, and a set of general stats: first-try wins, helps received, areas completed, collections completed and sets completed. The stats are running counts, with no badge or marker beside them.

### O29. Propeller Madness

After level 27, Royal Match presents Propeller Madness as a pop-up and as an item that stays on screen. It tells you to collect 5 propellers to win the reward and to complete all steps to win the grand prize, which is 10,000 coins over 2 days. Collecting 5 propellers completes the first step and opens the next, collecting 100 coins. Each completed step opens another with a better reward, ending in the grand prize, and you can collect the rewards from steps you've passed while the event runs. The propeller is also one of the game's board items, which erases a row or a column. With the doubling window running, step 6 is a few levels away.

### O30. The Egg Hunt

After level 38, Royal Match presents the Egg Hunt. It tells you that beating levels collects eggs and that 25 more eggs are needed to smash the next basket, over 2 days and 12 hours. The event also takes an item that stays on screen and fills as you play. Passing the hard level at 39 gives 30 points toward it, and tapping play on the event pop-up takes you into the next ordinary level rather than into a separate mode.

### O31. The Easter Pass

At level 37 the screen darkens and an arrow points at a key icon with a 27-day 12-hour countdown, introduced as the Easter Pass with a first reward to claim. The pass tells you to beat levels and collect keys to unlock rewards, and offers activation, which buys a second set of rewards at the same positions. The track holds 30 rewards, and each free reward has a golden-lane reward at the same position. The first position pays 1 TNT in the free lane and, in the activated lane, 8 lives instead of 5, a golden profile picture frame and golden username, and a gift for teammates. The second pays 1 coloured candy free and 15 minutes of infinite hearts in the activated lane. The final position pays 3 times power-ups and boosters free and, in the activated lane, 1 hour of infinite boosters plus 1,000 coins. Positions in between include a special chest, doubled power-ups and 30 minutes of TNT. A bonus bank sits at the bottom of the track, and says that activating the pass unlocks it at the end of the stages; you reach it by scrolling to the bottom. When you reach a position, the key icon carries a marker showing something is waiting, and the pass is also offered at the bottom of the lives panel with an activate button.

### O32. The Culinary Collection

After level 40, Royal Match starts the Culinary Collection, telling you to complete sets for amazing rewards, with 27 days and 12 hours to run, and gives you a first card pack of 4 cards. The collection holds 15 sets of 9 cards. The first pack gives a rolling pin, falafel, marshmallow and sorbet, 2 of them carrying 2 gold stars and one carrying 1. The collection's info says to collect card packs from events and chests, get cards from card packs and friends to complete sets, and complete all sets to claim the collection rewards and the chef badge. The sets are frozen, street foods, sweets, garnishes, tools, dairy, pastries, spices, cuisines, snacks, market, coffee, drinks, dining and breakfast. Duplicate cards become card stars, which Royal Match says are used to open chests. The grand prize is 10,000 coins, the chef badge and 10 times boosters, and each set shows the reward for completing all 9 of its cards. Completing area 3 gives 4 further cards.

### O33. The team tournament

After you join a team with enough members, Royal Match shows a team tournament icon and tells you to convert tokens to lances and contribute to your team. Royal Match shows the team's current position, the reward at the next position, each member's contribution and your own standing inside the team. The team shows at position 9 with 4 more tokens needed to reach the next reward, a power-up. Members with zero contribution are marked as getting no reward without contributing. After level 26 a reward is collected and 76 more tokens are needed to become a knight. After level 34 a further reward arrives, your armour changes from silver to gold, and you're shown in second place within the team. The tournament has 2 days and 13 hours remaining when the team offers appear against it.

At the teams item, Royal Match says you must reach level 21 to join a team. You can view a team before then, but tapping join gives a message to reach level 21. Before you're eligible, a team's page shows a description, capacity, team score and the level the team itself requires. The top team requires level 13,400 and is marked closed with high activity, and some teams say crowns are needed. You can't send a request while under level 21. The stated value of joining is free lives and free rewards, and the prompt to join arrives when you complete level 20, one level before you can act on it.

On the last item in the navigation bar, Royal Match says you need to reach level 41 to unlock the collection, and describes nothing else about it. The Culinary Collection itself begins once you complete level 40.

Royal Match locks its boosters and power-ups at the start and releases them at set levels, and locked items are shown but can't be tapped. The royal hammer comes after 6 or 7 games, the arrow after level 14, the cannon after level 16 and the jester's hat after level 18, each followed by a short lesson at the level where it first works.

### O37. 10 members needed

After level 22, Royal Match shows a team tournament pop-up with a notice that at least 10 members are needed in the group to take part, which your team doesn't have. Leaving a team asks for confirmation, and you hold only one team at a time, so you leave one before joining another. Once you join a team with enough members, the tournament appears. On the leaderboard's friends tab, Royal Match shows only a prompt to connect Facebook, and you can't reach the friends list without it. Declining the connection gives a message that the Facebook sign-in failed and to try again.

## Economy and resources

Royal Match has 2 earned balances, gold coins and gold stars, and hearts that refill on a timer. Each event also counts its progress in a unit of its own.

### O39. Gold coins

Royal Match grants gold coins from level play, from King's Nightmare, from bonus levels, from area chests and from event rewards, and holds them as a balance on the home screen. You spend them on a heart refill at 900 and on 5 extra moves at 900, and they're sold in packs for money. The balance stands at 2,093 after 2 levels. A King's Nightmare pays 50 coins, later doubled, an area chest pays 250, a bonus level pays more than 500, and event grand prizes are 10,000.

### O40. Gold stars

Royal Match grants 1 gold star for each level you clear and accepts stars only as payment for area tasks. With too few stars the task is blocked, and Royal Match says to beat levels to earn more. Tasks cost 1 star and 2 stars, and stars pile up if you don't build.

### O41. Hearts and limits

Royal Match holds hearts as a balance of 5, taken on failure, restored on a timer, bought with coins, and obtainable from teammates. The Easter Pass raises the limit to 8 when you activate it. The paid offers grant periods of infinite hearts rather than heart quantities, from 15 minutes up to 18 hours. Royal Match sends you to your teammates from the lives panel when you hold no free lives.

### O42. Event units

Royal Match runs several separate counting units, each belonging to one event and each moved forward by ordinary level play. Tokens convert to lances and go to the team, keys advance the Easter Pass, eggs advance the Egg Hunt, propellers and then collected coins advance Propeller Madness, and duplicate cards become card stars. ### O43. Crowns and team score

### O44. Chests and card packs

Royal Match gives chests when you complete an area and card packs in the Culinary Collection, and their contents differ between instances.
